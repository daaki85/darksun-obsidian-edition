# Lead with the outcome, layer the arithmetic

The combat logs players trust most share one shape. Each event gets a short line that leads with who did what to whom and what came of it. The arithmetic sits in a second layer the player can open. Colour says whether the event was good or bad for the party, and a glyph or word says the same thing again, so colour is never the only cue. Repeats are merged, misses are dimmed, rounds are marked, and a fight ends with a summary. The Obsidian Edition's dice log already beats its genre on the hardest part: it prints the target number (AC, "needs 4+", the percentage), which the Infinity Engine, Neverwinter Nights and KOTOR logs never did. Its weaknesses are presentation. One attack takes four to five lines. The verdict comes after the arithmetic. Colour marks the kind of line rather than its consequence. Its palette of eleven colours is more than readers can decode, and several pairs merge for colour-blind readers. Its THAC0 detail line prints bonuses as "+2" yet sums them to a smaller number, which reads as wrong arithmetic. The ranked methods below fix these in order of payoff. First, fold attack, damage and HP into one outcome-first line. Then rewrite the detail as a single left-to-right chain. Third, recolour by consequence with a five-role palette checked for contrast against #30304D. The rest are round rules, dimming, merging, filters and a per-fight summary. Each method is marked for the Tk widget, the 320x200 panel, or both. One caveat applies throughout. The session's network blocked page fetches for almost every game forum, wiki and accessibility site, so most game findings rest on search-engine snippets. Only the DCSS, NetHack and Brogue findings, which come from source code and docs read in full, can be called verified.

## Classic logs showed the roll but hid what it was rolled against

Classic dice CRPGs split into two families. The Gold Box used narrative two-liners in a column about 16 characters wide (text columns 23-38 of a 40-column screen), and each message overwrote the last ([wish-goldbox docs](https://wish-goldbox.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api/generated/automap.combatlog.html), snippet). A reviewer complained that messages "all appear in the same space and always overwrite each other" ([math.uh.edu review](https://www.math.uh.edu/~barrett/data/software/games/PoolOfRadiance)). The manual's only remedy was a speed setting: "If you are having trouble reading messages before they disappear, use the Slower command" ([Gold Box wiki](https://goldbox.fandom.com/wiki/Pool_of_Radiance/Manual), snippet). DOSBox players still find combat messages flash past at high cycle counts ([GOG](https://gog.com/forum/forgotten_realms_collection/text_scrolls_too_fast_por), snippet). That is the 320x200 constraint the panel inherits, and it is why the panel's scrollback and its never-waiting lines matter.

The Infinity Engine, NWN and KOTOR family went math-first: **`Paladin - Attack Roll 16 - 1 = 15 : Hit the Fighter - Damage Taken (7)`** in Icewind Dale: EE ([Beamdog forum](https://forums.beamdog.com/discussion/comment/1183580), snippet), and `9+4=13 *Hit*` in NWN ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/188666-neverwinter-nights/44912633), snippet, attribution unconfirmed). Both print the attacker's side of the sum and only a verdict for the defender's, so players reverse-engineer the AC in forum threads ([Beamdog "Combat log information"](https://forums.beamdog.com/discussion/68773/combat-log-information), snippet). Detail was opt-in and fragile. BG2's `ExtraCombatInfo` ini flag "would show you the THAC0 and Damage calculations (every single adjustment)", but it does nothing in BG:EE ([Beamdog](https://forums.beamdog.com/discussion/7518/editing-baldur-ini-extra-combat-info), snippet). BG:EE's "To hit rolls" toggle left some players seeing "only some of the hit rolls" ([Beamdog thread 34487](https://forums.beamdog.com/discussion/34487/not-an-issue-no-to-hit-roll-information-even-when-enabled-in-gameplay-gt-feedback), snippet). A log that silently drops lines erodes trust more than one that shows nothing.

Temple of Elemental Evil is the classic outlier players praise. Its "Rolls History" window shows each roll as a line, and clicking the blue entries opens "a breakdown of the dieroll + modifiers" ([GOG ToEE forum](https://gog.com/forum/temple_of_elemental_evil/need_advice_for_zombieslime_fight), snippet; [Before I Play](https://beforeiplay.com/index.php?title=The_Temple_of_Elemental_Evil), snippet). Players use such logs as audit tools: they check printed modifiers against the rules ([GOG IWD2 thread](https://static.gog.com/forum/icewind_dale_series/iwd2_attack_roll_modifier_wrong), snippet). So a log's arithmetic has to add up as printed. That is why method 2 below matters.

## Modern games converge on a verdict line plus an on-demand breakdown

Every well-documented modern game puts the outcome before the arithmetic and keeps the full derivation one step away. Owlcat's Kingmaker log gives each event one line. Its tooltip shows "all of the bonuses and penalties for the to-hit roll and their sources", and extra blocks appear only "if circumstances call for it", such as a crit confirmation or concealment ([Kickstarter update #37](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/owlcatgames/pathfinder-kingmaker/posts/1946988), snippet). Pillars keeps modifiers on hover ([Quarter To Three](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/the-spoiler-free-pillars-of-eternity-thread-about-managing-combat/76675), snippet). BG3 splits an entry into hoverable "Resulting Attack Roll / Resulting Defence / Resulting Damage" parts ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/4032473436330492524), snippet). Solasta lets players "hover any attack, damage, saving throw, ability check roll" ([Solasta patch notes](https://www.solasta-game.com/solasta-crown-of-the-magister/news/110-solasta-winter-update-patch-notes), snippet). A Pillars player wrote the whole chain unprompted as **"Accuracy 50, Deflection 60: -10. Hit Roll 70, -10: 60 -> Crit"** ([Obsidian forum](https://forums.obsidian.net/topic/78020-convert-graze-hit-crit/), snippet). Each step is labelled and an arrow points to the verdict, which is a plain-text format that evidently reads naturally.

The complaints are about how the detail is delivered, not about having it. Hovers fail ("Sometimes when you mouse over it, it just doesn't display", [BG3 Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/4032473436330492524), snippet). Tooltips cover other log rows, and Deadfire's were mispositioned as late as patch 3.0 ([Obsidian forum](https://forums.obsidian.net/topic/105457-ui-combat-log-tooltips-positioning-bug/)). Logs and tooltips disagreed with the real maths in BG3 ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/baldurs-gate/baldur-s-gate-3-s-spell-tooltips-have-been-lying-to-you-for-potentially-months-though-if-you-ve-been-running-off-the-chance-to-hit-you-re-just-fine)) and in Rogue Trader ([beta known issues](https://roguetrader.owlcat.games/beta_known_issues), snippet). BG3's log showed only the higher of two advantage dice ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/3802776599346312809), snippet). Solasta players asked for damage dice in the log rather than just totals ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1096530/discussions/0/3784750917889482533), snippet). Modders pulled both ways: a Deadfire mod hides buffs from the tooltip ([Out With The Good](https://www.nexusmods.com/pillarsofeternity2/mods/370)), while an exporter saves tooltip text to a file ([Combat Log Exporter](https://www.nexusmods.com/pillarsofeternity2/mods/103?tab=docs)). Readers differ, so verbosity should be a switch, and the mod's Least/Short/Detail levels and Tk "Show details" toggle are the right shape already. Two cheap grouping fixes recur. BG3's Patch 4 made the log split "combat rounds into separate headings" ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/137645/baldurs-gate-3-patch-4?amphtml=1), snippet), and Caves of Qud offers a separator line between combat rounds ([Qud wiki: Options](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Options), snippet). XCOM 2 and DOS2 show percentages without a roll log, and players there suspect the dice ([Steam XCOM](https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/412446292767037621), snippet). Printing the actual roll against the needed number answers that suspicion.

## Roguelikes and MMOs tame volume with merging, dimming and consequence colour

The roguelike sources are the strongest in this research, because they were read in full. DCSS merges identical messages within a turn: "The killer bee misses you. x5". It joins short same-channel messages with "; " only when they fit the line ([options_guide.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crawl/crawl/master/crawl-ref/docs/options_guide.txt); [message.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crawl/crawl/master/crawl-ref/source/message.cc)). Its shipped palette is just three semantic names, **`$danger := lightred`, `$warning := yellow`, `$boring := darkgrey`** ([standard_colours.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crawl/crawl/master/crawl-ref/source/dat/defaults/standard_colours.txt)). Hundreds of regexes map onto those three. Under "Unimportant messages and combat clutter", misses and zero-damage hits go to `$boring` ([messages.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crawl/crawl/master/crawl-ref/source/dat/defaults/messages.txt)). A forced `--More--` is kept for run-changing events (level-up, Zot warnings, falling into lava) and never for ordinary hits. NetHack offers the same controls as `MSGTYPE=hide|norep|stop`. It moves "you are low" out of the log onto the status line with `hilite_status:hitpoints/<=30%/red` ([Guidebook](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NetHack/NetHack/NetHack-3.7/doc/Guidebook.mn)).

Brogue adds the most transferable idea: **colour a combat line by who was hurt, not by what kind of line it is**. In `messageColorFromVictim`, harm to the player or an ally is `badMessageColor` (reddish), harm to an enemy is `goodMessageColor` (bluish), and a miss is plain white ([IO.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tmewett/BrogueCE/master/src/brogue/IO.c), [Combat.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tmewett/BrogueCE/master/src/brogue/Combat.c)). It folds same-turn messages with semicolons, collapses repeats to "(x3)" and then "(many)", and fades older lines toward black so "the bottom messages are the most recent". Cogmind's developer treats the main log as secondary, "showing only six lines at a time", with messages "like 4-5 words". His opt-in full combat log prints "the entire breakdown of every roll" as `Base Hit %: 60+0h+10m=69` ([Grid Sage Games](https://www.gridsagegames.com/blog/2014/02/message-log/), snippet; [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/722730/discussions/0/2290590708541447855/), snippet). That three-tier split maps directly onto this mod: the map shows the event, the panel names it, and the Tk widget explains it.

MMOs show what happens at scale. WoW keeps the raw stream for add-ons (`COMBAT_LOG_EVENT_UNFILTERED`) and filters only the view ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_2.4.0/Consolidated_API_changes/Guide_to_the_New_Combat_Log), snippet). Players read the aggregates add-ons build instead: per-fight segments, death recaps, and floating text that merges AoE "into one event with cumulative damage" ([Warcraft Wiki: Damage meter](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Damage_meter), snippet; [MSBT](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/mik-scrolling-battle-text), snippet). Skada and Recount time a fight differently, so the same log yields different numbers ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-03-12-addon-spotlight-skada-versus-recount.html), snippet). A summary has to define when a fight starts and ends. Dwarf Fortress is the cautionary case. Sparring floods a capped buffer of about 3,000 entries ([DF bug 10480](https://dwarffortressbugtracker.com/view.php?id=10480), snippet), and players read the wound screen rather than the log ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/975370/discussions/0/3727323721768168342/), snippet). Detail without a summary stops being read.

## Legibility numbers: what the palette and the panel can carry

The accessibility guidance converges, though its primary pages were snippet-only here. XAG 102 asks for **4.5:1** contrast for information-carrying text and 3:1 for inactive elements. Over a non-solid background, contrast is measured against the lowest-contrasting area ([XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102), snippet). WCAG 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for non-text marks such as rules and icons ([U. Iowa SC list](https://itaccessibility.uiowa.edu/wcag21SC)). GAG says "ensure no essential information is conveyed by a fixed colour alone" ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/), snippet). About 8% of men have red-green colour deficiency ([Colour Blind Awareness](https://colourblindawareness.org/?p=409)). Categorical accuracy holds to about six categories and then falls ([arXiv 2508.17460](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17460)). Readers take in roughly the first two words of a list item ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/first-2-words-a-signal-for-scanning/)), so the verdict belongs in the first words. Butterick's 45-90 characters per line and 120-145% leading set the Tk targets ([Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/)).

I computed WCAG ratios for the mod's current colours against #30304D. **Every one passes 4.5:1.** The lowest is the monster red #FF7070 at 4.71, then saves #86ABFF at 5.61 and turns #D3A28A at 5.62. Contrast is not the problem. Colour count and colour-blind separation are. Today a line can carry a kind colour plus two name colours drawn from eleven hues. A Machado-matrix simulation (my own computation, ΔE*ab below 15 counted as confusable) finds several collapsing pairs. Under deuteranopia, attack green #8FD16A merges with monster red #FF7070 (ΔE 11) and with peach member #FFB86B (11). Damage amber #FFA600 merges with "other" yellow #E7EB18 (13). Cyan member #6FD8E8 merges with misses #B4B4C8 (11). Under protanopia, green and peach collapse (ΔE 5), and saves blue and the magenta member collapse (3). Member 4's #FFFFFF is barely distinct from body text #D7D7E3 for anyone.

The panel has its own measured limits, which I took from `dsloader/combatlog.py` and the game's font in a playtest install. The font is **9 px high** and proportional. Digits are a fixed **6 px**, so numbers already stack like tabular figures, and the narrow letters `i` and `l` are 2 px. The text runs from x 11 to about x 166 of the 180 px panel, roughly **155 px or ~28-30 characters**. "Seneca hits Arena Guard for 11" measures 156 px and just fits. The current Short line "Seneca attacks Arena Guard: 15 vs 4+ HIT, 11 damage (1d4+1)" measures 314 px and wraps to three rows of a six-row panel. Panel colours are snapped to the nearest index of the area's palette and drawn on a mottled well. That makes XAG's worst-background rule binding. Against the lighter stone #454561, the dim tier #A0A0B8 drops to 3.61 and salmon #FF8A80 to 4.04.

## Twelve ranked methods for the two logs

The ranking weighs readability gain against evidence strength and cost. The first four change what every line says and how it looks, and they carry most of the benefit.

| Rank | Method | Fits | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One outcome-first line per attack (attack + damage + HP folded) | Both | NN/g first two words; every modern log; Cogmind's 4-5 words |
| 2 | Detail as one left-to-right chain whose arithmetic adds up | Tk (panel Detail) | Pillars player chain; IWD2 audit threads; BG3/RT log-vs-maths complaints |
| 3 | Colour by consequence, five roles, contrast-checked | Both | Brogue victim colour; DCSS three semantic names; ~6-category limit |
| 4 | Redundant non-colour cues (glyph prefix, fixed outcome word) | Both | GAG, WCAG 1.4.1, XAG 103 |
| 5 | Dim the inconsequential (misses, zero damage, no-effect saves) | Both | DCSS `$boring` defaults; Brogue white misses |
| 6 | Round rules and indented sub-events | Both | BG3 Patch 4 headings; Qud separator |
| 7 | Merge repeats within a turn ("x3") under strict rules | Panel (Tk optional) | DCSS x5; Brogue (x3)/(many); DF request |
| 8 | Channel tags and incoming/outgoing filters; filter the view, not the record | Both | WoW filtered/unfiltered; DCSS channels; FFXIV/GW2 |
| 9 | Per-fight summary and a death recap | Tk (one line in panel) | WoW meters; DF lesson |
| 10 | Panel width budget and name shortening | Panel | Measured font widths; Gold Box column |
| 11 | Recency cues and pause only for rare events | Panel | Brogue fade; DCSS force_more; GAG own pace |
| 12 | Tk typography: leading, hanging indent, tab stops, scalable size | Tk | Butterick; WCAG 1.4.8/1.4.12; Wrath font-size complaints |

### 1. Fold each attack into one outcome-first line (both)

Today one attack yields four or five lines: attack, THAC0 sum, damage, save, and HP. Emit one event line whose first words are actor, verb and target. The verb is the verdict, followed by the damage and the target's HP state, then a dimmed clause with the roll. Use one fixed order so the eye learns its positions. For the Tk widget:

```
● Seneca hits Arena Guard for 11 — backstab   19/30 HP    d20 15 vs 4+ (85%) · AC 8
● Seneca misses Arena Guard                                d20 3 vs 4+ (85%) · AC 8
○ Arena Guard hits Seneca for 6                22/38 HP    d20 17 vs 11+ (50%) · AC 2
○ Arena Guard saves vs Sleep: no effect                    d20 14 vs 13+
● Seneca kills Arena Guard (11 damage)         +120 XP
```

The template is `{pip} {Actor} {verb} {Target}[ for {N}][ — {tags}]{TAB}{Target HP}{TAB}d20 {r} vs {need}+ ({pct}%) · AC {ac}`. Verbs come from a closed set: hits, misses, crits, fumbles, kills, saves, fails, resists, casts. Tags (backstab, crit, from behind, two weapons) follow an em dash only when they changed the result. The weapon and its dice go to the detail layer, since the summary says what happened and the detail says why. For the panel at the Short level, use `{Actor} {verb} {Target} {N}` plus at most one tag:

```
Seneca hits Guard 11 backstab        (153 px)
Seneca misses Guard (3 vs 4+)        (151 px)
Guard dies. Seneca +120 XP           (136 px)
```

The roll pair moves to the panel's Short line only for misses and saves, where it answers "why didn't that work?". The Least level drops it entirely.

### 2. Make the detail a single chain that adds up (Tk; panel Detail level)

The current THAC0 line, "THAC0 19, +2 from behind, +2 backstab, +2 STR, +1 weapon = 12", prints plus signs that reach a smaller number. A reader checking the sum gets 26, and auditing is exactly what log readers do. Print the modifiers with the sign they have on THAC0, or name them as to-hit bonuses and subtract once. Write each derivation left to right, in the order it was computed, ending in an arrow:

```
    to hit: THAC0 19 −2 behind −2 backstab −2 STR −1 weapon = 12;  12 − AC 8 → needs 4+;  d20 15 → HIT
    damage: 1d4 [3] ×2 backstab = 6;  +1 weapon +4 STR 18 → 11
    Bone Hayneedle +1 (1d4+1)
```

This mirrors the chain a Pillars player wrote unprompted and Cogmind's `60+0h+10m=69`. The damage line also fixes today's order, "+3 backstab (the dice 3 x2 = 6)", which states the result before the operation. Use the Unicode minus and multiplication signs (− ×) in Tk, and ASCII `-` and `x` in the game font. Follow Owlcat's rule: add a sub-line only when a special step happened (a crit confirmation, magic resistance, a reroll), and always keep every die rolled (BG3's lost advantage die is the warning).

### 3. Recolour by consequence with five roles (both)

Replace the six per-kind line colours with roles that answer "good or bad for us?". Apply the role to the verb and the number, keep the rest of the line in body text, and keep names as their own bold channel. The proposed values, checked by my computation against #30304D:

| Role | Hex | Contrast on #30304D | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body text | #D7D7E3 | 8.89 | everything not below |
| Good for the party | #6CC6FF | 6.74 | party's hits and damage, foes' failed saves, heals |
| Bad for the party | #FF8A80 | 5.56 | damage to members, members' failed saves, a member down |
| No effect / detail | #A0A0B8 | 4.96 | misses, zero damage, no-effect saves, the detail lines |
| Notable | #FFE066 | 9.73 | kills, crits, level-ups, XP, a member under 30% HP |
| Round rule (non-text) | #7E7EA4 | 3.27 | separator lines only (WCAG 1.4.11 needs 3:1) |

Good and bad stay apart under all three simulated dichromacies (smallest ΔE 51, ample). Every text role clears 4.5:1 on #30304D. Blue for good and salmon for bad follows Brogue's own pair and the Okabe-Ito blue/vermilion axis ([Okabe-Ito reference](https://conceptviz.app/blog/okabe-ito-palette-hex-codes-complete-reference)). For member names, the remaining hue clashes are with the outcome roles, not among the roles themselves. Change member 4 from #FFFFFF to a hue clearly apart from body text. Move member 1 from cyan, which sits too close to the good blue, to #8FD16A (6.94), which today's attack colour frees. Give monster names a muted #E0B0A0 (6.58) so red stops meaning both "monster" and "harm". Even then, names and roles sum to about ten hues, past the six-category limit. So in the panel, show identity by the existing filled/hollow pip and the name word, and use colour only for consequence. In the panel, verify each role after palette snapping against the lightest mottle of the well. Where the well reaches #454561, the dim tier falls to 3.61, so flatten the well to one dark shade under the text rows, or lighten the dim role there. Extend the tests' `TEXT_PAIRS` check to these snapped panel pairs.

### 4. Never rely on colour alone (both)

Keep a non-colour cue for every role. The Tk widget should prefix lines with the panel's ● (a party member acts) and ○ (a foe acts). The verdict should be a fixed word from the closed verb set, so "misses" reads as dim even in monochrome. A "!" before the target's HP when a member drops under 30% repeats NetHack's threshold cue. Keep one capitalised token at most (HIT, CRIT). XAG 101 prefers sentence case over all-caps lines ([XAG 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/da-dk/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101), snippet), so the rest stays sentence case. In Tk, put the summary's names in bold, as today. In the panel, don't use bold, since a +1 px stroke closes the 9 px font's counters. Let colour and the pip carry identity there.

### 5. Dim what changed nothing (both)

Following DCSS's shipped defaults, draw misses, zero-damage hits, saves with no effect and the detail lines in the dim role. They stay in the record but read as background. The hits, harm and kills, which change the fight, then stand out by lightness as well as hue, a cue that survives every colour deficiency.

### 6. Mark rounds and indent what belongs to an event (both)

In Tk, start each round with a rule in #7E7EA4 carrying the round and the order, `──── Round 3 · Seneca, then Guard, Cilla ────`, with 6 px of space above (Tk's `spacing1` on the rule's tag). Indent detail and save lines under their event with `lmargin1`/`lmargin2` of one em, as "Show details" lines are today. In the panel, the header already says the round. Add a single 1 px rule in the separator colour between rounds inside the well. It costs one pixel row, not a text row of nine. Don't add blank lines in the panel, since six rows are too few to spend on whitespace.

### 7. Merge repeats within a turn under strict rules (panel; Tk optional)

Merge two events only when they share the same round, actor, verb and target, and the verb is miss, no-effect save or zero damage. The result is `Guard misses Seneca x3` (116 px). For hits, merge only with the sum spelled out, `Cilla hits Guard x2 for 4+6`, and never across actors, which is the failure DCSS's own code calls "a hack". Above five, write `(many)`, as Brogue does. In Tk, keep every line by default and offer merging as an option: the Tk log is the auditable record, and folded lines cannot be audited.

### 8. Tag every line with a channel and filter the view (both)

Give each Tk line Tk tags by channel: `party-acts`, `foe-acts`, `miss`, `damage`, `save`, `spell`, `kill`, `status` and `out-of-fight`. Offer checkboxes that hide tags with `elide`, so the find box still searches everything. That is WoW's filtered/unfiltered split: filter the view, never the record. The panel's All/Party/Damage/Saves filters are good. The one gap the MMO evidence points to is **incoming**, "what is hurting us", answered in FFXIV and GW2 by colour or side ([GW2-SCT](https://github.com/Artenuvielle/GW2-SCT)). The Party filter shows the party's own actions, so add a "Hurt us" filter, or redefine Damage to put harm to members first.

### 9. End each fight with a summary and a death recap (Tk; one panel line)

When a fight ends, write a block to the Tk log. It should give, for each member, damage dealt and taken, hits/attacks, kills and saves made/failed, followed by the last three events before any member fell. Define the fight as the game's own fight flag on to off, so the numbers can't drift the way Skada's and Recount's do. The panel gets one Notable line: `Won: 4 kills, party took 37`. DF shows the alternative, players abandoning the log for state screens.

### 10. Budget the panel line to 155 px (panel)

Write the Short and Least templates to fit one row. Shorten foe names to their last word when that is unique in the fight ("Arena Guard" becomes "Guard"), and number duplicates ("Guard 2"). Drop "for" and "damage", since the number after the target is the damage. Leave the dice expression to the Detail level. Where a line still overflows, keep the hanging indent the panel already uses, but cut a tag before wrapping. A line of two rows halves what the six-row panel shows. Semicolon folding of different events (Brogue) rarely fits: "Seneca hits Guard 11; Cilla misses Guard" is 195 px. Use it only when the joined line measures 155 px or less.

### 11. Show recency and pause only for rare events (panel)

Draw lines from earlier rounds one step darker, staying at least 4.5:1 after snapping, and keep the current round at full strength. That is Brogue's fade, minus the fade to black, which would fail contrast. Keep "never waits for a click" for ordinary events. If a pause is ever added, reserve it for DCSS-class events (a member killed, a level-up) and make it optional, which follows GAG's "progress through text prompts at their own pace".

### 12. Tune the Tk typography (Tk)

Keep TkFixedFont, whose fixed digits align the numbers. Set tab stops so the HP and roll columns of method 1 line up, as tables right-align numbers ([Matthew Strom](https://matthewstrom.com/writing/tables)). Add 2-3 px of `spacing3` between lines for about 130% leading, inside Butterick's range. Keep summary lines under about 90 characters so the roll clause doesn't wrap at the window's default width. Bind Ctrl+plus and Ctrl+minus to the font size and remember the choice. Wrath players found the log too small and too large in the same thread, and its slider "doesn't work for me" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1184370/discussions/0/4132682093267216698), snippet), so a size control that works is worth more than the right default.

## Conclusion

The mod's combat log is unusual among dice CRPGs: it already shows what the classics hid, the number each roll had to beat. What remains is ordering and economy. The verdict goes first, an event becomes one line, colour means one thing, and the detail is arithmetic a reader can check. The roguelike evidence, the only part read at source here, points the same way as the snippet-level CRPG evidence: a small semantic palette, dimmed non-events and merged repeats keep a stream readable at any size.

Three things deserve testing in play, because no source measured them. Does consequence colour beat identity colour for this party's players? Does folding attack and damage into one line still let rules-minded players audit a backstab? Does the 155 px panel hold a full round of four attackers at the Short level?
