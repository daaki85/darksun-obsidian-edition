# Class outfit workflow

Use this sequence when taking a playable race from its original Shattered Lands map figures to equipped party sprites. Keep the roster, comparison images, and open art decisions together on the race’s gallery page. Apply the [human figure review checklist](#human-figure-review-checklist) to every new model, including both figures and every playable single-class and multiclass outfit.

| Stage | What to make | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish the roster | Copy the legal single, two-class, and three-class choices from the player’s guide. Use the character-creation names in captions. Check which sexes are available at character creation and record each playable figure’s walk and combat picture IDs. The Mul has one male entry. | Check each combination against the multiclass table. A check mark beside two single classes does not establish that the pair is playable. Humans use separate dual-class rules. [Races](../../PLAYER-GUIDE.md#races) · [More than one class](../../PLAYER-GUIDE.md#more-than-one-class) |
| 2. Build the bodies | Put the original, cleaned base, and minimally dressed figure side by side for every native pose. Preserve the head, body proportions, foreground hand, and where each foot meets the ground. Redraw the anatomy exposed by removing stock garments. Compare the shading on each reconstructed area with surviving skin in that pose. | Step through both figures independently. Look behind the arms for lining, between the legs for transparent holes, and along the feet for stock footwear. Compare the last walk step with the first. |
| 3. Establish the single classes | Start with an exposed torso, an outfit with shoulder pieces, and a cloth-heavy outfit, as the race allows. Compare them beside an original map figure and a finished human on the same terrain at game size. Then finish the remaining legal classes. | Check the face above the collar, shoulder placement, front-to-back color, and hems through a turn. Use highlights and shadows from the game palette. Review the base and three silhouettes together using the [first fitting review](#first-fitting-review) before drawing the remaining singles. |
| 4. Propose the multiclasses | Choose one single-class silhouette for each legal combination. Borrow a visible detail from the other class, such as a hood or sash. Compare each pair with both singles and each triple with all three two-class parents in the same pose. | View the variants together at native size. Check the width, contrast and attachment of the borrowed detail against the fitted parent at 1×, especially from behind and during a strike. An arm can legitimately hide part of a sash. Keep costume details separate from inventory items. |
| 5. Inspect motion | At the end of each stage for singles, pairs and triples, export a labeled, synchronized loop and a still sheet for both figures with a fixed ground line. Check all native poses, including bow, hit, and fallen. Repeat walk and strike cycles when inspecting their transitions. | Watch once at game size for flicker and sliding. Then step frame by frame at integer enlargement for holes, stray pixels, cut-off limbs, a bow hidden by cloth, and garments that flatten into stripes. Record the figure, class, direction, and step for each correction. Fix shared faults on the body or shared garment and rerender its dependent outfits. |
| 6. Verify equipped play | Record the approved art revision. After integration, align the study and empty-slot Loader output on the same ground anchor and compare palette indices and transparency for all poses. Account for intended Loader features such as pointed ears. Then create or load examples of each supported class combination. Compare an empty slot, the equipped item, and the slot after removal on both figures. Follow the project’s [playtest procedure](https://github.com/daaki85/darksun-obsidian-edition/blob/claude/class-outfits/playtest/PLAYTESTING.md#startup-procedure). | Check each visible foot with boots, shield grip through a swing, cloak coverage from front and rear, and armor recoloring on the intended garment. Compare mundane and magical armor in the same pose. Watch a full shine cycle for spill onto skin, hair, or cloak. Verify the base outfit returns on removal and the stock appearance when the display option is off. |

**Review fit and material detail before moving to the next outfit.** For every race, show the original playable figure, the study and a retail Shattered Lands figure wearing a comparable material in the same pose, at native scale on the same ground. Use the retail Warrior for worn leather, the Priest for robe folds and the Thief for a trailing cloak. Repeat on a second floor. Compare the size of the highlights, the shadow under a fold, and the strength of the outer edge. Watch the full walk and a strike at 1× before zooming in. A strap should still meet its belt when the ribs turn, a cap should follow the shoulder under the hair, and a hem should lift with the knee while the foreground hand crosses it. Record the reference and the pose that made the fit easiest or hardest to read. Apply the same comparison when a combined class or an equipped item changes the visible material.

Publish the current sheets and loops with a short status line identifying an art proposal, a loader-rendered preview, or a verified in-game capture. Keep the pose caption visible so feedback can identify a frame precisely.

For a borrowed Thief hood, carry the main garment’s dye through the lit crown, middle folds and dark face opening. On a Fighter scale, pick up the surrounding cloth color in the shadow and reserve a short worn-bone highlight for the ridge. Use a fixed floor and camera for the before-and-after loop. In front, rear and profile, follow the join to the jerkin and the highlight on the bare upper arm through all four steps. Check how much of the cap is visible as the elbow passes the ribs. [Five-figure shoulder walk](shoulder-walk.gif) Check all three bow poses against the bare deltoid. When the elbow rises, the edge flattens and the near hand can hide part of the fastening. On a triple, trace the third class’s belt, lining or sash separately. Compare at 1× beside the retail Thief, Priest and Warrior. [Before and after on five races](multiclass-materials.png) · [Watch all 29 poses](multiclass-materials.gif)

## First fitting review

Use the exposed torso, shoulder armor and robe to check the new figure before borrowing their shapes for other outfits. Start with front idle, a rear step, the widest profile stride, a strike with the head lowered and a drawn bow. Then run the same comparison through every native pose, including recoil and fall.

| Compare on the same floor at game size | What to trace at pixel scale |
|---|---|
| Retail figure → clean body → dressed figure | Follow the cheek into the lower lip, jaw shadow and throat. From behind, follow the nape into the shoulders. Toggle the outfit on the body and look for a collar, cap, hood edge or bright fleck that makes the chin disappear. A comparison limited to the marked head pixels missed this on the Mul. Trace the face from the retail cel and set the garment opening separately on the clean body, including where a wrist passes the cheek. [Mul neckline comparison](mul-neckline.png) |
| New figure → fitted parent → retail figure in motion | Locate the new figure’s shoulder, crossing forearm, waist and advancing knee on each cel. Watch where a sleeve ends and where the hem lifts, then follow both back to idle. On the Mul, the borrowed sleeves extended past the rear elbows and the profile hem followed the human stride. Check for isolated cloth pixels inside the silhouette as well as holes through it. [Mul robe in motion](mul-cleric-fitting.gif) |
| Finished skin, leather and linen beside retail materials | At 1×, check that the face separates from the collar, bare limbs turn from light into shadow, and the garment reads against sand and paving. Enlarge to inspect folds, hide edges and highlights, then replay at 1× for flicker. Save a labeled close-up of the hardest overlap alongside the full-pose sheet. |

Use the clean body to mark where each garment touches the shoulder, ribs and hip. Reuse those anatomical points across classes and trace each armhole, fastening and hem for the garment that owns it. Using the painted Mul Cleric robe to place three other outfits gave a tunic and two capes the same fit. Compare their silhouettes together in front idle, rear walk and profile stride before carrying those garments into multiclasses. [Mul fitting comparison](mul-singles-fit.gif)

When two leather outfits blend into the same skin tone in profile, line up their previous and revised versions with the finished human and retail Warrior. Trace the near shoulder into the armhole, and the belt into the flap over the leading thigh. Check the same landmarks again on paving. On the Mul, the borrowed human leather covered too little of the turning torso even though the face and silhouette checks passed. [Six-class fitting review](mul-fitting-details.png)

When shortening a cloak borrowed from another figure, compare the unclothed torso, the donor outfit and the fitted result in front idle and through a crossing-arm pose. Trace the fabric between the outer folds down to the belt. On the male half-elf Druid, both edges moved correctly even though the middle of the garment was missing. [Half-elf Druid comparison](half-elf-druid-fitting.gif)

If the defect starts on the clean body, correct the body first. If dressing it causes the defect, correct the garment, then replay every single and multiclass that inherits it. During equipment review repeat the difficult pose with the relevant slot empty, equipped and removed.

## Human figure review checklist

Use these checks during body cleanup, outfit review, and equipment testing. The human examples identify where a defect was easiest to see. Inspect the equivalent pose on each new figure, then inspect the rest of its frames. Use the finished [female](README.md#female-human) and [male](README.md#male-human) human loops as references.

### Body base and motion

- [ ] **Stop after a walk.** Play front idle → four front steps → front idle on a fixed ground line. The early male idle had a larger head, wider shoulders, darker skin, and a different stance. Check the head, shoulder width, skin ramp, and planted foot against that model’s original frame. Repeat from behind and in profile.
- [ ] **Check features added by the loader.** Keep a retail reference and a preview with the loader’s features on the same pose. On elves, check the pointed ear against the cheek in profile, the long hair from behind, and the raised hand and bow during combat. Some bow and hair pixels share the same gold. Use position and overlap to tell them apart.
- [ ] **Strip the default outfit on every frame.** On the man, the black riveted harness returned as soon as the Fighter or Gladiator started walking. On the woman, black cloak lining left wedges beside the arms and hips, the Gladiator retained the stock top and red/magenta gems, and an orange neck gem survived in side and melee views across classes. The hit pose retained a black cloak and one blue boot. Compare the original, cleaned body, and dressed figure in the same pose. The female review flagged roughly 8–9% black in front views versus 2% on the man. Use that discrepancy to locate leftover lining, and judge each outline against its own figure. Trace suspicious colors back to their source. Green belongs on the Druid and Ranger, and dark outlines belong where the anatomy calls for them.
- [ ] **Check the body with the cloak off.** Inspect the bust, torso depth, shoulders, arms, hands, hair, hips, knees, and heels against the original from each direction. The human woman lost bust definition, hair and arm pixels were missing on the Fighter and Gladiator, and the uncloaked rear idle exposed an awkward hip-to-leg join. Check the least-covered class first and correct shared anatomy on the base.
- [ ] **Inspect newly exposed skin at game size.** Put the original, cleaned body, least-covered legal outfit and a fully dressed class on the same floor at 1×. Check whether you can still follow the shoulder, ribs, hip and bent knee beside the clothed figure. Where a harness, cloak or trousers covered the anatomy, use the surviving shoulder, upper arm or thigh on that figure as the first reference. Compare a similarly lit retail figure when the garment hid the whole area. Trace the light over the shoulder blade, ribs and hip, then down the thigh to the bent knee. Follow each highlight through idle → walk → idle, strike and recoil. Look for straight bands across a turning torso, knees that lose their shadow and scattered highlights that flicker. Use the skin ramp and keep small smooth planes where the original does. A count of uniform 2×2 squares can locate a suspect pose. Cloth speckle targets do not apply to skin. Verify a correction on the bare layer and where that skin shows between straps, a kilt or a short cape. [Eight-figure skin comparison](body-texture.md)
- [ ] **Follow each foot through the rear walk.** On the woman, the right foot appeared to turn into the left leg. Step from rear idle through steps 1–4 and back to idle, tracking each heel and toe. Inspect rear step 4 and side idle against contrasting ground for missing edge pixels. The Gladiator exposed gaps that other clothes hid.
- [ ] **Inspect the whole outline and its interior.** At integer enlargement, look for detached pixels, transparent pinholes, abrupt edges, and gaps in continuous cloth. Preserve deliberate openings between an arm and the torso or between the legs. Male trouble spots included the Fighter’s front idle and bow, Gladiator front walk 2, mantle rear walks 1 and 3 and rear strike 2, Ranger side walk 1, and Druid rear strike 2 and bow. On the woman, inspect the first side strike and the bow pose where the Cleric draws across her body. Check the fallen pose for a return to stock clothing.

Compare the dressed and cleaned pixels inside the silhouette as well. In the Mul’s second side strike a strip of skin divided the Druid’s mantle even though the complete figure had no transparent hole. Follow the cloth from its shoulder attachment past the crossing arm to the hem. Check a suspected pinhole or isolated clothing pixel against the retail wrist, face and knee in that pose before repainting it. [Druid side strike](mul-druid-side-strike.png)

### Outfit and palette at game size

Compare every outfit, on both figures, beside original Shattered Lands figures and a finished human on light and dark game terrain. View at 1×, then 2× or 3× to locate a fault. For human references, the game’s own Fighter (picture 1252), Thief (630), Cleric (1563), and Mage (724) show how much shape and shading survive at map size. Use exact game palette entries and compare the exported pixels. Keep class colors consistent across figures, retain the original skin tones, and check contrast across the full roster. Each outfit needs a recognizable outline and a readable area of color from the front, side, and rear.

| Human outfit | What to inspect wherever this class or detail appears |
|---|---|
| Fighter (Warrior) | The red-brown leather jerkin should cover the stock harness while walking. Bone shoulder caps and hip flaps should break up the outline. Keep the neckline below the face, as with the Cleric. Track the woman’s bone pieces across all four rear steps for sliding or changes of side. |
| Thief | Show pants or shorts clearly below the waist, including in a stride. Separate the slate hood and short cloak from dark leather with a midtone. Check the hood’s profile and the cloak below long hair from behind. |
| Cleric (Priest) | Keep the face clear above the collar. Use the long cream robe and shaded folds to convey cloth texture. In side strike 1, the forward-swinging panel should stay cream through the swing. Inspect its edge in the bow pose for holes. Watch the front and rear walk at 1× for two dark folds tracking the thighs from belt to hem. The halfling robe needed a continuous lit panel over the stride and a crease offset toward the side seam. |
| Preserver (Mage) | The selected human design is the plum **mantle**. The full robe was an earlier silhouette study. Give the mantle depth over the shoulders and down the back, texture in its folds, and a highlight that survives dark terrain. Compare front idle and front walk directly with the rear view to catch pale mauve blending into skin. |
| Druid | Keep the green shoulders and ochre kilt legible with a few broad marks. Watch the shorter cloak through rear idle → walk → idle. Its hem should settle in folds and follow the hips through the transition. The woman’s idle hem had an abrupt horizontal cut. |
| Ranger | Follow the strap, quiver, hood, or green-brown cloak in profile, where a shield can disappear behind the body. Inspect the rear cloak’s width below the hair and the angled folds at rest. |
| Gladiator | The exposed chest, harness or arm guard, and bone or bronze shoulder piece should read through every step and strike. Check the exposed arm and the outer torso for lost pixels or leftover stock clothing. Separate dark red leather from skin in rear and side views. Follow the shoulder guard into its strap and the strap into the waistband on the opposite hip. Check the kilt against the advancing thigh in profile and the bow hand as it crosses the belt. [Male Gladiator fit](human-gladiator-fit.gif) |
| Psionicist | Give the collar or sash enough contrast to show at 1× while keeping the brow and cheeks readable. The early dark straps disappeared against the stock harness. Follow the identifying color around the back and check that a multiclass garment leaves it visible. |

- [ ] **Compare the face with the original, one pose at a time.** At game size, check the eyes, nose, beard or cheek shadow, and chin. Zoom in and alternate the original and dressed frame on the same baseline. The dwarf Cleric’s collar rose across the jaw, the front-facing Thief’s hood filled the opening, and Psionicist trim crossed the brow on dwarves and humans. Keep the Cleric’s robe below the chin, open the Thief’s hood from brow to jaw, and put the Psionicist’s accent on the garment if there is too little room above the eyes. Recheck the low head in recoil and the foreground hand and bow in combat. Repeat with ordinary and magical armor and a cloak to catch equipment masks touching the face.
- [ ] **Check the bare face before approving the body.** Alternate the retail head and the cleaned head on the same ground line, with a magnified crop centered on the lower face. Trace the eyes, mouth highlight, and jaw shadow through idle, wind-up, follow-through, bow, and recoil. On both elves the body redraw reached the mouth. The female elf’s face drops below the estimated head rectangle in front strike 2. Keep those native pixels out of cloth, armor, and cloak masks, then repeat with an open hood. Check the helmet independently when a bow tip rises above the crown. On halflings, compare the head again after stripping wristbands. A wrist passing the temple can put face pixels inside the removal mask. Check the shoulder cap and exposed strip of neck in the same pose. [Elf face comparison](elf-faces.gif) · [Halfling face comparison](halfling-face-fit.png)
- [ ] **Compare each hood with the uncovered hair in the same pose.** Follow the cloth from crown to rim to nape on both figures and every hooded combination. Side idle and all four side steps reveal a hood that follows the exact slope of the hair. Use the rear, wide strikes, hit and fallen poses to find locks that swing free. On the floor tile at 1×, follow the nape into the cloak through all four rear steps. Check whether dark hair reads as a hole, and whether a lock breaks through the crown during a strike or recoil. Equip a differently colored cloak and check the rim, exposed locks and face opening again. [Human Thief example](human-thief-hood.gif) · [Six-figure comparison](thief-hood-comparison.gif)
- [ ] **Check the garment from the side.** Compare each side step, strike, and bow with the side idle. On a Cleric, trace the robe from below the chin to the belt, including the cloth above and below a crossing wrist. On a Thief, trace the fold from the crown down the back of the neck into the shoulder at game size. Look for a skin-colored gap as the dwarf leans forward and check where the guard or bow hand overlaps the cloth. Keep the eyes, cheek and chin visible. Follow the two ends of the front hood through the walk and strike to catch a band forming under the jaw. Compare multiclass hoods with their own tunic or sash at game size. Repeat on both figures and every build that inherits the garment.
- [ ] **Check the ground behind a moving robe.** Play side idle → four steps → side idle at game size. At integer enlargement, trace the cloth behind the arm and over each advancing knee. The human Priest had enclosed terrain pixels in steps two and three. The female elf Cleric had a single opening behind the wrist in step four. Compare suspect gaps with the stock stride: the opening below a raised leg should lead back out to the ground. [Human Priest loop](human-priest-profile.gif) · [Elf pose sheets](elves.md)
- [ ] **Compare each combined outfit with its parent on the same ground.** Check the approved small pale caps on the dwarf Cleric/Fighter’s cream robe. Follow the dwarf Fighter/Psionicist’s narrow teal sash from the front and rear, then watch the side walk for jerkin coverage. Track the hem over each thigh and look for rectangular shadows that fill the space beside the hip.
- [ ] **Follow each shoulder guard through a full turn and swing.** Locate the upper arm on the original before drawing the plate. At 1× the light should turn over the deltoid into a short shadow against skin or leather. Check the four steps, wind-up, follow-through, and bow on both figures. Shorten or hide the far plate when the arm disappears. Repeat on every legal combination that inherits a guard, collar, hood, or sash, including triples where those pieces meet.
- [ ] **Follow the cloth as the figure turns or raises an arm.** The woman’s Thief, Mage, and Ranger need cloak visible below the hair. Also check the Fighter and Gladiator from behind for a class detail visible beside or below the hair. Compare Mage and Ranger rear idle with their walking folds for evenly spaced “barcode” stripes. On Thief, Mage, Druid, and Ranger, compare bow and hit poses with the original figure’s side panels. Check the first side strike for ground-colored holes. Preserve the foreground hand, bow, and intended cloth behind them.
- [ ] **Compare cloth texture at map size.** Put the previous outfit, dressed figure, and retail Priest (1563), Thief (630), Mage (724) or Warrior (1252) on the same terrain at 1×. Enlarge using nearest-neighbor pixels. Trace the shadow side and hem of pale linen on sand, the crown and neck fold of an open hood, and the widest trailing panel in the middle of a stride. Follow the same fold between frames to catch flicker. In profile, compare the shoulder and trailing hem separately through steps 1–4 and back into step 1. Watch where the corner lifts away from the calf, reaches farthest behind the body, and drops. Check the open ground under the hem on both the class garment and equipped cloak. Compare the lifted hem with the same pose rendered without the mantle. Inspect the thigh and the space between the legs for an old hem that still takes the cloak dye. [Male elf example](elf-mantle-midstride.png) [Retail and Preserver walk comparison](elf-mantle-swing.gif) Count flat four-pixel squares on the same cloth mask before and after. Compare isolated pixels and color counts with the retail material, allowing small straps and exposed skin to retain their own shading. Repeat on the legal pairs and triples. [Worked comparison and measurements](cloth-detail.md)
- [ ] **Check for floating garments against a retail figure on each art pass.** At game size follow the same fold from the nape or shoulder to the free corner through idle, the four steps and the return to idle. Compare the hip with the fitted parent in that pose. When the corner swings outward, the kilt and belt should continue to follow the pelvis. [Half-elf walk beside the retail thief and mage](half-elf-hooded-cape-walk.gif)
- [ ] **Review motion and explain the preview.** Show both figures, label each outfit and pose, and identify whether viewers are seeing base colors, equipped renders, or gameplay. Play every direction and action and provide stills for individual frames. Compare the final sprite on game terrain with its design drawing to catch details that disappeared at native size. Inspect mirrored side views with asymmetric gear. The engine mirrors the arm along with the figure, so the relevant check is whether the item stays attached to that arm.

### Equipped play

- [ ] In side idle and all four steps, trace the cloak below the swinging elbow to the hem at 1×. Check class colors and equipped dye, especially when the near arm crosses the waist. Look for enclosed terrain pixels between lining and calf. Follow the breastplate reflection through front, back, and profile walks. Toggle the plate in each step. Follow the original upper arm all the way to the wrist and check the belt below it. A single steel or glint pixel visible through a wrist or on a buckle can make the plate float even on a connected silhouette. [Female elf example](elf-woman-plate-fit.png) [Cloak loop](cloak-stride.gif) · [Plate loop](plate-stride.gif)

- [ ] Hold each pose while the item's palette cycles. Compare a magical piece with a plain copy through the same motion. Check both visible wrists and feet separately when a boot or cuff shares colors with the underlying sprite. Compare front strikes, rear bow and the upside-down fall with the empty pose. Keep the bow in front of the cloak. Look for a bright line that bridges a turning shoulder and the chest. [Magical equipment in motion](magical-equipment.md)
- [ ] Compare the same native cloth pixels with empty slots, full leather, full plate, both named robes and a magical chest. Follow the light and shadow of a seam through all 29 poses. Find an original-colored collar, strap, hem, crest or sash on each equipped single. Compare every equipped pair and triple with each equipped parent in the same pose. Inspect the weakest poses at 1×, especially where only one or two pixels carry a class color. Check sparse shine on partly hidden cloth, face and wrist crossings, ink outlines and the foreground bow. For studies without fitted slots, label the dye surface as an estimate. [Worked armor comparison](armor-dyes.md)
- [ ] Compare the uncovered outfit, chest plate, complete plate, and magical set at 1× on terrain. Follow edges from chest to arms and legs, then compare the rear mantle between renders. Try each named robe with its actual inventory record. Check its binding, lower folds, glow and removal. Compare both glove weapons on the hand that actually swings, the two exposed boots, and the difference between a bracer and a glove. Include long and short weapons on a canvas extended below the feet, the dwarf recoil, the great-axe head through the swing and the sling or chatkcha at the walking hip. Inspect single classes first, then pairs, then triples. Compare each pose with the same pose in every immediate parent outfit. Judge the borrowed detail at game size on the floor. A few different pixels can vanish against the parent garment. Trace the trailing edge of a profile cloak across the full walk, especially when the arm swings behind the torso. Check the hood on the fastest swings to see where moving hair should emerge from under it. Check enclosed terrain at pixel scale against the original pose before closing a gap between limbs. For weapon clipping, render once with room beyond all four edges, including below the soles. Compare that render with the game's picture bounds.

For each new model, record which figures, outfits, poses, and equipment states were inspected, along with any open defect and a labeled frame that shows it. Mark in-game checks pending until they have been exercised in play. After a shared fix, rerender every affected class and multiclass and replay the transition where the fault first appeared.
