# Class outfit workflow

Use this sequence when taking a playable race from its original Shattered Lands map figures to equipped party sprites. Keep the roster, comparison images, and open art decisions together on the race’s gallery page.

| Stage | What to make | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish the roster | Copy the legal single, two-class, and three-class choices from the player’s guide. Use the character-creation names in captions. Record each figure’s walk and combat picture IDs. | Check each combination against the multiclass table. A check mark beside two single classes does not establish that the pair is playable. Humans use separate dual-class rules. [Races](../../PLAYER-GUIDE.md#races) · [More than one class](../../PLAYER-GUIDE.md#more-than-one-class) |
| 2. Build the bodies | Put the original, cleaned base, and minimally dressed figure side by side for every native pose. Preserve the head, body proportions, foreground hand, and where each foot meets the ground. Redraw the anatomy exposed by removing stock garments. | Step through both figures independently. Look behind the arms for lining, between the legs for transparent holes, and along the feet for stock footwear. Compare the last walk step with the first. |
| 3. Establish the single classes | Start with an exposed torso, an outfit with shoulder pieces, and a cloth-heavy outfit, as the race allows. Compare them beside an original map figure and a finished human on the same terrain at game size. Then finish the remaining legal classes. | Check the face above the collar, shoulder placement, front-to-back color, and hems through a turn. Use highlights and shadows from the game palette. Review the base and three silhouettes together before propagating edits. |
| 4. Propose the multiclasses | Choose one single-class silhouette for each legal combination. Borrow a visible detail from the other class, such as a hood or sash. Compare each triple with its nearest two-class design. | View the variants together at native size. Check whether a player can still see the distinguishing detail from behind and during a strike. Keep costume details separate from inventory items. |
| 5. Inspect motion | Export a labeled, synchronized loop and a still sheet with a fixed ground line. Check all native poses, including bow, hit, and fallen. Repeat walk and strike cycles when inspecting their transitions. | Watch once at game size for flicker and sliding. Then step frame by frame at integer enlargement for holes, stray pixels, cut-off limbs, a bow hidden by cloth, and garments that flatten into stripes. Record the figure, class, direction, and step for each correction. Fix shared faults on the body or shared garment and rerender its dependent outfits. |
| 6. Verify equipped play | After integrating outfit selection, create or load examples of each supported class combination. Compare an empty slot, the equipped item, and the slot after removal on both figures. Follow the project’s [playtest procedure](../../../playtest/PLAYTESTING.md#startup-procedure). | Check each visible foot with boots, shield grip through a swing, cloak coverage from front and rear, and armor recoloring on the intended garment. Compare mundane and magical armor in the same pose. Watch a full shine cycle for spill onto skin, hair, or cloak. Verify the base outfit returns on removal and the stock appearance when the display option is off. |

Publish the current sheets and loops with a short status line identifying an art proposal, a loader-rendered preview, or a verified in-game capture. Keep the pose caption visible so feedback can identify a frame precisely.
