How the game works

Part of Obsidian Edition's documentation: the original game's own rules, for the curious, and the detail of one rule change (Spells and psionics as the manual describes them). The README is about the mod.

What the game does that its manual doesn't say, from its code and checked in play. The dice log shows each as it happens, and the rule changes change some of it. Several findings were first pointed out by the notes of yuan-ti, CalvinRodo's new engine for the game, and checked here in the game's own code. One section is the mod's own: the detail of Spells and psionics as the manual describes them, beside the game's spells and powers.

Character creation

Every click of the die on the creation screen (and every change of race or sex) rolls a new character. From the game's code, and checked against the screen:

  • Abilities. Each score is rolled four times as 4d4 + 4 (8 to 20), the best of the four kept, and the race's adjustment added. The game then raises the class's prime requisite to 17 and every other score to 9 (clerics, fighters, preservers, thieves), 12 (druids, psionicists), 13 (gladiators) or 14 (rangers), CHA included. (With the Loader: a score is raised only to the class's minimum in the manual: cleric WIS 9; druid WIS 12, CHA 15; fighter STR 9; gladiator STR 13, DEX 12, CON 15; preserver INT 9; psionicist CON 11, INT 12, WIS 15; ranger STR 13, DEX 13, CON 14, WIS 14; thief DEX 9; a multiclass character the highest of its classes' minimums.)
  • Race adjustments (STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA): dwarf +1 −1 +2 0 0 −2; elf 0 +2 −2 +1 −1 0; half-elf 0 +1 −1 0 0 0; half-giant +4 −5 +2 −5 −3 −3; halfling −2 +2 −1 0 +2 −1; mul +2 0 +1 −1 0 −2; thri-kreen 0 +2 0 −1 +1 −2. Humans have none.
  • Hit points. One die for every level of every class (d8 clerics and druids, d10 fighters, gladiators and rangers, d4 preservers, d6 psionicists and thieves), doubled for half-giants. The total is divided by the number of classes (rounded down), and then CON's bonus is added for every level: a warrior's full bonus (+1 at CON 15, +2 at 16, +3 at 17, +4 at 18, +5 at 19-20, +6 at 21-23, +7 at 24-25; −1 at 4-6, −2 at 2-3, −3 at 0-1), and at most +2 for levels in the other classes. There is no automatic maximum at first level: the first level rolls like the rest.
  • Choices without dice. Raising a score to a new class's minimum (adding Thief raises DEX to 9) and PSP aren't rolled, so they don't appear in the log. The creation screen's other random numbers only choose pictures.
  • The total. After the six scores comes what they add up to, the most they may add up to with Scores and hit points as rolled.

Changing sex or race can make the game roll hit points twice, once with the old scores and once with the new: the last hit point line is the one that counts.

Initiative

From the game's code: at the start of every round each combatant's initiative is 20 + a roll of 0-9 + a DEX adjustment + Hasted +2, Slowed −2 and Blind −2. The DEX adjustment has its own table: −6 at DEX 1, −4 at 2, −3 at 3, −2 at 4, −1 at 5, none for 6-15, +1 at 16, +2 at 17-18, +3 at 19-20, +4 at 21-23 and +5 at 24-25. The weapon makes no difference (there are no weapon speeds). The highest score acts first; a second roll, 0-199, decides between equal scores (the log shows it only for those). Choosing Wait lowers the character's score to 10 (or by one, if it's 10 or less already) so they act later in the round.

No critical hits

A natural 20 on an attack always hits, and a natural 1 always misses, but that is all the d20 does: the game has no critical hits or fumbles. Its attack routine uses the d20 only for those two checks and the comparison with THAC0, and never passes it to the damage routine, so a hit on a 20 rolls the same damage as any other. Two things multiply a blow's damage by a whole number: a backstab, and a helpless target (Enlarge scales it by 10% a caster level).

Helpless targets take double

A creature that is Paralyzed (Hold Person, Hold Monster, a sand howler's gaze, Blood Flow...) or Out Cold takes double damage from every weapon blow that hits it, and makes no saving throw at all: it fails each one without a roll. The game's attack routine hands its damage routine two numbers, the damage and twice the damage, and the damage routine takes the second when its target is helpless; the save routine fails a helpless target before rolling. The x2 comes after a backstab's multiple (a backstab x3 on a held target is x6) and before Biofeedback's 2 off. The blow still has to hit: the d20 is rolled as ever (a held target is no easier to hit), and the manual says nothing of any of it. A spell's damage comes with its own second number, the most its dice can do, which a helpless target takes instead.

With Held targets as in AD&D ticked (the default) the mod has such a blow hit automatically for normal damage instead, as AD&D's 2nd edition does.

The dice log shows the blow's x2 (x2 Paralyzed: a helpless target takes double), and a Poisoner's hit on a helpless target, whose poison has no save to show, as is poisoned by the hit: Poison (10 damage), no saving throw (Paralyzed), followed by the 10 when it lands.

The game's two weapons

The manual says a character with two weapons ready uses the second "at a disadvantage", unless a ranger or dextrous. The game's code does something else: with two weapons ready (in melee), every attack, first hand and second alike, is adjusted by the DEX table used for initiative with its sign flipped and never below 0, and rangers are left out. That comes to a bonus of +6 at DEX 1, +4 at 2, +3 at 3, +2 at 4 and +1 at 5, and nothing at DEX 6 and up, so in practice there is no off-hand penalty at all: both weapons hit as well as a single one would. The log names it, e.g. +2 two weapons at DEX 4. It looks like a sign slip: AD&D uses the same DEX adjustment to make two-weapon fighting harder at low DEX.

Attacks from behind and backstabs

From the game's code:

  • An attack is from behind when the attacker stands in the square directly behind the way the target is facing. A creature faces nowhere in particular at the start of each round; the first attack on it in the round turns it to face that attacker (one of eight directions), and it keeps facing that way for the rest of the round. So a second attacker on the far side, later in the same round, attacks from behind. (Its own attacks don't turn it.) It gets +2 to hit, and the target loses its DEX bonus and its shield. The game counted a missile from behind the same way, at any distance (an archer across the room); with the Loader a missile is never from behind by the target's facing, only as a hidden archer's shot, "unseen" (the stealth rule: Hiding in shadows to backstab).
  • A backstab is an attack from behind by a thief, in melee, with a weapon that isn't too heavy (the game's weight value at most 40, a metal long sword's). It gets another +2 to hit (+4 in all), and on the thief's first two attacks of the round (an off-hand one too) the damage, STR bonus included, is multiplied: x2 at thief levels 1-4, x3 at 5-8, x4 at 9-12 and x5 from 13 (AD&D's table). (With the Loader: the first attack's dice only.)

Which weapons can backstab, the game's and the Loader's (magic ones included). Weight belongs to the weapon's type and material, in the game's units, which look like tenths of a pound (a dagger is 10, a club 30, a mace 100, as AD&D's 1, 3 and 10 lb), so the limit is 4 lb. Missile weapons (slings, bows, a thrown chatkcha) never backstab: it has to be melee.

The game's saving throws

From the game's saving throw routine. The spell names which of the character sheet's five saves to use; a natural 1 always fails and a natural 20 always saves (in the patched game, unless the save can't be made: below); otherwise the d20 and the modifiers below must reach the save's number.

Petrification/polymorph for almost every spell. The game's code maps the spell's save kind 5 to the sheet's third save; kind 1 is paralysis/poison/death, used by the clouds, Poison, Slay Living and the psionic attacks. The spell save, the one AD&D uses for spells, is never used: a 3rd-level warrior needs 13 against Psychic Crush (paralysis) and 14 against Fireball (petrification), where the spell save would be 16.

Fire, cold and electricity: the d20 counts double. Each spell's record has a word of flags saying what kind of damage it does, and the saving throw code doubles the d20 whenever the kind is fire, cold or electricity. Nothing else is doubled: not acid (Acid Arrow), crushing (Ice Storm, Magical Stone), poison (Cloudkill), draining (Vampiric Touch, the Cause Wounds spells) or the psionic attacks. The doubled spells are:

  • fire: Burning Hands, Flaming Sphere, Fireball, Flame Arrow, Minute Meteors, Fire Shield, Wall of Fire (both), Focus Heat, Produce Fire, Flame Strike;
  • cold: Chill Touch, Cone of Cold;
  • electricity: Shocking Grasp, Lightning Bolt.

In between a 1 and a 20 the doubled roll makes saving far easier: needing 14, a normal d20 saves 35% of the time and a doubled one 70%, which is why Fireball's victims usually get away with half damage. It isn't AD&D, and the game never explains it; it may have been meant as a dodge. The log says it on each save: d20 = 7, doubled against fire = 14. The mod's Saving throws against spells puts the spell save back and takes the doubled d20 away.

The target's spells and effects:

  • Blessed +1, Barkskin +1, Spirit Armor +3 (but not on paralysis/poison/death saves), and the Save penalty effect −1.
  • Prayer: +1 if its caster is on your side, −1 if not.
  • Protection from Evil +2 against an evil caster (lawful, neutral or chaotic evil); Protection from Fire and from Cold +3 against fire and cold spells; Protection from Lightning +4 against electricity.
  • +4 against a spell aimed at one target (not an area) when the caster can't see you: the caster is Blind, or you're Invisible (or Invisible to Undead, against an undead caster) and the caster can't detect invisibility.

Class, race and abilities:

  • WIS, against mind-affecting spells, charms and holds, fear and illusions: −6 at WIS 1, −4 at 2, −3 at 3, −2 at 4, −1 at 5-7, +1 at 15, +2 at 16, +3 at 17 and +4 at 18 and up.
  • CON, on paralysis/poison/death saves: −2 at CON 1, −1 at 2, +1 at 19-20, +2 at 21-22, +3 at 23-24 and +4 at 25. Dwarves and halflings also add CON x 2 / 7 (+1 for every 3.5 points); in AD&D that bonus replaces the adjustment (with the Loader: CON as in AD&D).
  • Druids +2 against fire and electricity; psionicists +2 against mind-affecting spells and charms.
  • Some spells carry a modifier of their own (a monster's poison at −4).

Spells with rules of their own: creatures of 6th level or lower can't save against Cloudkill; against Chaos only warriors (fighters, gladiators and rangers) can; against Dismissal the target adds its level and takes away the caster's. But "can't save" is −100 on the save, and the game compares the total with the number needed unsigned (79BBAh), so a total such as −85 reads as 171 and the creature always saves (seen in DOSBox: Arena Guards "unable to save" took half Cloudkill's damage). The patched game's compare is signed, so "can't save" fails, even on a natural 20 (which the game took as a save before adding the −100), whatever the Options (The game's bugs, fixed). Against Scare no one saves at all: its record allows no save, so the saving throw ends at its first test and the rules the save code has for Scare never run. Those rules: 6th level and up always save and everyone below can't, but for some elf or half-elf priests (AD&D gives elves, half-elves and priests a bonus), which it asks for as a creature that is both an elf and a half-elf, so no one qualifies. The patched game allows the save, so the rules run (6th level and up always save; below 6th, no save, the signed compare making the −100 fail), and lets an elf or half-elf priest save: The game's bugs, fixed.

Rules in the code that never come into play: Cloak of Bravery's +4 against fear applies only to a kind of spell that no spell in the game is marked as (the patched game marks the fear spells: see The game's bugs, fixed), and so does AD&D's DEX defensive adjustment for attacks that can be dodged (+5 at DEX 1 to −6 at DEX 25 on AC, so −5 to +6 on the save), unless the rule change puts it on the fire, cold and electricity spells. And nothing in the game gives saves from items: there are no rings or cloaks of protection, which is why the mod adds two rings and a cloak. Their +1 is in the log's saving throws as +1 Ring of Protection and +1 Cloak of Protection (with AD&D's rules for them off, all of it as Ring of Protection).

Monsters' defences

From the game's damage and fighting code; none of this is in the manual:

  • Every creature has a monster kind, and each kind a resistance class and a set of properties, in a table the game fills when it starts.
  • Every hit has damage kinds: fire, cold, electricity, acid, poison, draining, psionic, death, and for weapons crushing, edged or pointed, from the item's type; a natural (unarmed) attack's is crushing, fists and monsters' claws alike (with the Loader, a party thri-kreen's claws and bite, and Erdlu Claw's talons, are edged: Erdlu Claw). A weapon's hit also carries its magic: one bit for +1 or better, one for +2 or better and one for +3 or better (the weapon's plus, or its ammunition's if that's higher). A monster's own attacks count as magical by its level: (level - 2) / 2, so a 6th-level monster hits like a +2 weapon.
  • A resistance class is up to four rules: "these kinds of damage: this percent of it". The largest percent that applies counts. So "crushing, edged and pointed: 0%; +1 or better: 100%" is a monster only magical weapons hurt. The game's 14 classes come to: only +1 (or +2) weapons hurt it, sometimes with immunity to poison and draining, or to fire; half damage from non-magical weapons and psionic attacks (the Rampager); immune to crushing weapons (the mastyrials); immune to crushing and pointed weapons, fire and acid, so that only edged weapons hurt it (the slimes); immune to fire and cold (and half from electricity); half from fire; immune to poison; immune to psionic attacks.
  • Properties: can't be charmed or held; unaffected by spells left on the ground (fogs, clouds, walls, Web, Grease); not held by Grease, Web, Entangle, Solid Fog or Quicksand; and hits that also cast one of the monsters' powers on the target: 2d6 cold, 2d6 or 20 acid, paralysis, poison of 10 or 30 damage, a deadly Poison, disease on 1 hit in 10. The 20 acid (the Rampager's) and a corroding touch (the Babau's) can also destroy a worn item (see Items saving against acid). With the mod's monsters as the manual describes them, some of these differ (which): the shadows drain STR in place of cold and paralysis, the Babau and the Blue Slaad carry the deadly Poison (which kills on a failed save, in a fight too) in place of the corroding touch and the disease.
  • Undead (race 9 on the character sheet) take nothing from poison and draining, and mind-affecting spells, charms and holds don't work on them.
  • The game makes the gear of the people the party fights (Kurzak's armour and sword, the Arena Guards' bows) in their backpacks, and puts it on only at each one's first action in a fight: weapons in hand, armour worn. Until then each is AC 10 with its armour unworn, so an opening blow (the game starts the fight after it), a backstab, or a first round's blow on one who hasn't acted meets no armour. Once on, a weapon in hand is fought with (in place of the first two natural attacks) and worn armour counts. The game never puts on rings, cloaks, belts, boots or amulets. (With the Loader: worn from the start.)

The dice log notes a weapon hit that takes less than its roll, or nothing at all, with what the monster's defences say about it (Mastyrial takes none of the 6 damage: crushing weapons can't hurt it: the game's mastyrials take nothing from clubs and maces, only from edged and pointed weapons).

Spells and effects

What the log says about spells comes from the game's own spell records and code, checked by casting each spell in a fight. Where the game differs from the AD&D rules, the log follows the game.

Damage. Each spell's record gives its dice: base dice plus dice (and a flat bonus) for each step of caster level, counted up to level 10. So Fireball and Lightning Bolt do at most 10d6, and Burning Hands 1d3 + 2 a level. A save halves the damage, or stops it all for spells such as Chill Touch. A creature that is Out Cold gets no save and takes the most the dice can do.

No spell misses. The game rolls no attack for a spell: Acid Arrow, Turn Pebble to Boulder, Magical Stone, Condense and the touch spells (Chill Touch, Shocking Grasp, Vampiric Touch, the Cause spells, Poison) always land, whatever the target's AC. Its code has a place for a to-hit test (the routine applying a spell to its target calls one for the missile spells, 78BA0h), but the routine there always answers "hit". With the Options tab's Spells roll to hit ticked (the default) they roll against the target's AC as a weapon does.

Saves. Almost every spell is saved against with petrification/polymorph, and against fire, cold and electricity the save's d20 counts double: see The game's saving throws.

How long. A duration is (caster level × so much + dice) × a unit of time. A round is 60 game seconds. Some effects last a number of uses instead (charges):

  • Stoneskin: 1 a level + 1d4. Any damage uses a charge, even fire that gets through, but only blows are stopped.
  • Ironskin: 1d6 blows stopped.
  • Mirror Image: 1 a level + 1d4 images. They stop no weapon blow, and take a spell for one creature three times in four, whoever cast it, so Armor cast on oneself is lost, and an image with it. (With the Loader: each hit and each foe's spell.)
  • Invisibility: one attack or hostile spell ends it. Improved Invisibility is timed instead, so attacking doesn't end it.
  • Minor Spell Turning: one spell turned back.
  • Armor: a number of damaging hits, one charge each (with the Loader: see The game's bugs, fixed).

Charm, Feeblemind, Web and a few others last until removed.

Other things the game does its own way:

  • Strength adds 1d6 to STR while it lasts, up to 24 (with the manual's spells 1d8, 1d6 or 1d4 by class, and not added to Adrenalin Control's: the bigger counts). The same goes for the psionic Adrenalin Control, and Weakened or lending strength takes it away (never below 3). A second Strength replaces the first.
  • Chill Touch takes 1 STR (Weakened) from a creature that fails its save, but the spell's time is 0, so the effect ends at once (with the manual's spells, an hour).
  • CON 20 or more gives a hit point back every 5 minutes of game time, at any such CON (AD&D: one an hour at 20 to one every 10 minutes at 25; with the Loader: CON as in AD&D). Every party member gets a psionic strength point back every 5 minutes.
  • Enlarge adds 10% a level of its caster to the whole of a melee hit's damage, STR's bonus and the weapon's plus included, rounded down (with the Loader: only the weapon's dice).
  • Shillelagh, Flame Blade and Spiritual Hammer need an empty hand: with a weapon ready the game says "Failed, weapon in hand" and nothing happens.
  • Death spells (Slay Living, Dismissal) do the target's HP + 10 on a failed save.
  • In the arena, summonings fail ("Your summoning goes unanswered").
  • A spell's caster level is the caster's highest level in a class that shares a sphere with the spell. Priests have one element each (cleric, druid and ranger classes come in air, earth, fire and water): Flame Blade, Focus Heat and Flame Strike are fire spells, Blood Flow and Dehydrate water, Deflection air, while spells such as Bless, Barkskin or Spiritual Hammer belong to every sphere. Cast by a priest of another element, an elemental spell counts caster level 0. The game shows each priest only their element's spells, so this only happens if a character somehow has the others.
  • Clerics never have the common 4th and 5th-level priest spells: Cure Serious and Critical Wounds, Raise Dead, Neutralize Poison, Free Action and the rest are marked for druids alone (a cleric's 4th and 5th levels are its element's spells only). The mod's Healer kit gives them to a water cleric.

The spell's area catches its caster too. Cilla's Scare made her Afraid, and her Fireball, cast at a Slig next to her, killed her.

Turn Undead

A cleric's Turn Undead is a button on the USE screen (the spell 190), not a spell: it takes no slot. The button shows for a character whose sheet has a cleric's element (air, earth, fire or water); druids have none, so they never get it. It tries each undead creature (race 9) in its area, one at a time, in a fight or out of one (out of one, it starts a fight). For each the game rolls a d20 and needs 10 + 3 x (column - row) or more:

  • the column from the creature's hit dice: 1 to 4 as they are, 5 and more one further (5 HD is column 6), at most 13;
  • the row from the priest level (the best of the cleric classes): 1 to 10 as they are, 11 counts as 10, 12 as 11, 13 and more as 12;
  • a 22 is made 20; anything over that can't be rolled, and 1 or less always works.

On the number or more the creature is destroyed (the game does it 1000 damage). Otherwise nothing happens: no "turned" (fleeing) result, nothing remembered. The same cleric can try again on its next turn, on the same creature too, in that fight and in later ones; the use ends the cleric's turn. Its description says "May be used once per creature": the game doesn't keep to that. Magic resistance is checked first; there is no saving throw.

Against AD&D's turning table (2nd edition), the steps of 3 are the same, but:

  • AD&D has a row between 1 and 2 HD (zombies) and one between 5 and 6 HD (ghasts). The game's 2 HD uses the zombie row and its 3 HD AD&D's 2 HD row (both one easier than AD&D's 2 and 3-4 HD rows), and its 5 HD the ghast row (one harder than AD&D's 5 HD row); 1, 4 and 6-11 HD are AD&D's rows.
  • AD&D's T (turned: the undead flee) and D (destroyed) are all destroyed in the game, and D* (2d4 more of the kind destroyed) is one.
  • AD&D's levels 12-13 share a column; the game gives level 13 the 14+ one (one easier).

The game's undead (their sheets in SEGOBJEX.GFF): Zombie, Convert and Tara's Follower 2 HD, Shadow 3 or 6, Skull Elder 4, Shadow Guard, Shadow King, Shadow Prince, Shadow Worker, Aposs, Cragg, Tanelyv, Tristram and Tynan 6. A 3rd-level cleric needs 7 against a Zombie and a 20 against the 6 HD ones (a 1st or 2nd level cleric can't turn those at all).

The dice log shows each try: Seneca turns Zombie: d20 = 12, needs 7+ (priest level 3 vs 2 HD) -> destroyed (code: DSUN.EXE 7ADB7h, the number at 7AE01h-7AE48h, the d20 at 7AE4Dh).

With "Spells and psionics as the manual describes them" (the default) all of the above is the game's rule only with the box unticked. Ticked, the mod takes over where the game works out its number (7AE01h) and uses AD&D's turning table instead (below). How it fits the game: a turned undead is made Afraid with the effect Cause Fear gives (the game's own way of adding it would refuse it to the undead, who are immune to fear spells); a priest who failed against an undead and tries it again ends the try at once, the turn spent, with no roll; the failures are forgotten when the next fight's first round starts, and by a turning out of a fight, which starts a new one (so a failure from out of a fight is forgotten as that fight's first round starts). Magic resistance is still checked first, by the game. The dice log says the number, the roll and the outcome (... needs 10+ (priest level 3 vs 2 HD, AD&D's table) -> turned: flees 3d4 = 7 rounds (Afraid)).

Psionics

Psionic powers are numbered after the spells (Detonate 138 to Thought Shield 171) and go through the same code: the same records for damage, saves and effects, so the log treats them like spells and names them. Some things are their own:

  • Level. A power works at the character's psionicist level; anyone else with psionic powers counts as level 1.
  • PSP. A power costs its PSP to use. Kept-up powers (Inertial Barrier, Biofeedback, Graft Weapon...) cost more PSP at the start of each round, and the game puts them on again for another round; when the PSP runs short, the power drops. The log shows every change in a party member's PSP (K'ratchek spends 18 PSP (52 -> 34)).
  • The power check. Each use rolls a d20 that must come up at or under the power's score: the ability the power uses (CON for most psychometabolism and psychokinesis, WIS for telepathy, Disintegrate and Enhanced Strength, INT for Superior Invisibility, Mind Bar and Synaptic Static) plus the power's adjustment, from −1 (Lend Health) to −7 (Mind Blank). A 1 always works and a 20 always fails; a failure says "psionic power check fails" and costs half the PSP. The game also keeps a number for each power a character knows, and adds anything above 1 to the score, but no code was found that raises it. Near someone else's Synaptic Static, the user first rolls a d20 against that number (the game reads the ability for it and drops it, so it's 1 for most characters): over it, "psionics disrupted by static". Otherwise the Static's keeper rolls a d20 at or under their own Synaptic Static score, and no lower than the user's, to disrupt it. The log shows each of these rolls.
  • Detonate (the daggorans use it too): besides 1d10 from the blast, it picks the worn item that breaks most easily, the one with the lowest fragility number (under 6: a cloak 3, a bone helm or leather chest armour 4, bone scale arm and leg armour 5; a short sword's 6 is never picked; on a tie, the first), and destroys it outright on a d20 under 15 less its plus (70% for a plain item; one of a stack). The log shows the check.
  • Psionic defence. Attacked by a psionic attack mode (Psychic Crush, Ego Whip, Id Insinuation, Psionic Blast), a character defends with WIS − 7, or with a defence mode already raised (used beforehand, it stays up at no cost each round) if that does better: the mode's rank plus half its cost, which the character pays again, if they can. A monster defends with its level where that's better. Two rolls of 1 to that number, both over the attacker's own roll for the power, resist it.
  • Mind Bar adds 75% magic resistance against mind-affecting spells: charms, holds, Scare, Confusion, Chaos, Feeblemind, Minor Malison. The log's magic resistance line counts it, and Lower Resistance halving the result (with the manual's spells: 30% + 1% a caster level off instead).
  • Body Weaponry makes unarmed attacks 2d4; Animal Affinity at least 1d10. Only the first of a creature's natural attacks: a thri-kreen's four claws, not its bite.
  • Monsters use them too: the Screamer Beetle's special attack is Psychic Crush (1d8, save vs paralysis/poison/death for half).

How the game works out thief skills

The game never shows thief skills, but it rolls them: for traps, and for the locks, walls and so on its scripts ask for (see Where the game rolls thief skills). The game gives the skills no names; its eight are AD&D's in AD&D's order (pick pockets, open locks, find/remove traps, move silently, hide in shadows, hear noise, climb walls, read languages), which the checks below fit. The roll is a d100 that must come in under the skill's chance. The game's code works the chance out as:

  • a base for each skill, in that order (28, 18, 13, 28, 18, 23, 78, −4),
  • plus 4 for each thief level,
  • plus a racial adjustment. These are AD&D's, for example a dwarf gets +10 to open locks, +15 to find traps, −10 to climb walls and −5 to read languages.
  • plus DEX: −5 for each point below 12, 11, 12, 13 or 11 (the first five skills); +5 for each point above 16, 15, 17, 16 or 16; and −3 for each point above 21, 20, 21, 19 or 19, so very high DEX gains less,
  • minus an equipment penalty (5, 0, 0, 10, 5, 0, 10, 0) when the thief has anything at all in the leg armour slot, the quiver or either hand. That is the game's own check: it doesn't look at what the item is (leather or metal) and ignores chest and arm armour and helmets, so a thief holding any weapon pays it. (The manual's "anything other than leather-type armor" is AD&D's rule, not what the code does.) (With the Loader: no equipment penalty.)
  • plus the situation's bonus or penalty (a hard lock, say).

With Thieves as in AD&D ticked (see Thief skills from AD&D's table), the first two and the DEX part are AD&D's instead.

Only characters with thief levels have the skills; everyone else's chance is 0. The character's condition must be Okay (the status the character screen shows under HP): a thief who is Stunned, Out Cold, Dying and so on can't use the skills. A character not yet played (New, before the game starts) counts as Okay with the mod, so a party's thief skills show while it is made (see More characters).

Some effects rule out a skill:

  • Blind, Afraid, Confused, Berserk and Paralyzed stop them all, except that a blind thief can still hear noise.
  • Slowed stops picking pockets only.
  • Fire Shield stops picking pockets and hiding; Mirror Image stops hiding.
  • Graft Weapon stops picking pockets, opening locks and climbing.
  • Feeblemind stops reading languages.
  • Enlarge scales the situation's bonus or penalty, not the skill: for hiding it's divided by (100 + 10 × Enlarge's level)%, for climbing multiplied by it. With no bonus or penalty it changes nothing; with a penalty, climbing gets harder.

Two effects make a skill certain instead: Detect Traps (anyone, thief or not, finds traps) and Invisible (hiding in shadows).

These effects work on the situation's bonus: ruling a skill out takes 1000 off it, so the roll can't succeed, and making it certain adds 1000. (One more rule in the code would make move silently certain, and hearing noise impossible, for someone wearing one particular item on their legs, but the game passes that check its two lists the wrong way round, so it never applies.)

Where the game rolls thief skills

There is no hide or sneak command, and nothing in the game's code rolls a thief skill on its own: every roll comes from the game's scripts (conversations, doors, walls...), which ask in two ways. These are all of them:

  • 13 skill checks: find/remove traps 4 times (a hidden passage, a secret door, a loose rug, a cord on a lava-dome egg; bonuses −10 to +3), open locks 4 times (a safe, a grate and cell doors; −4 to +8), climb walls 3 times, and pick pockets and hear noise once each (a key in a trustee's pocket; two men arguing by a wagon, a check for the whole party). Most are made by the character who acted.
  • 50 trap triggers, in 16 scripts. A script sets off an object at a spot on the map (a trap, or a blast: the bound prisoners in the arena, a Drajian messenger's thrown sphere, a summoning circle, a breaking mirror...). First the party's best at find/remove traps rolls, with no bonus; success, or Detect Traps on the character who set it off, avoids it. This is the roll the arena prisoner makes.

A party check is meant to go to the member with the best chance, but the game's pick compares the wrong way round, so the first party member always rolls (with the Loader: the best). (The game also has a script command that lets each member try in turn, but no script uses it.)

The game never rolls move silently, hide in shadows or read languages, so on their own they make no difference, nor does the equipment penalty on them. The Loader rolls them: move silently when a pocket isn't picked (Picking pockets), hide in shadows and move silently in fights (backstabs), and read languages when a Shinobi reads a scroll. The scripts also make 3 ability checks (a d20 under the ability): CHA twice and STR once.

python -m dsloader checks lists them all with the script's text around each (spoilers).

(The Loader's Characters tab shows each thief's chances as they stand.)

Resting

A rest (at a fire ring, or one a script gives) passes 8 hours and fills hit points, PSP and spell slots (the mod's Resting as in AD&D heals by the priests' spells and a night's natural healing instead), and the party's priests tend everyone: if the best cleric among those Okay is 5th level or more, it casts Cure Disease, Remove Curse and Remove Paralysis on each party member, and so does the best druid; a druid of 7th level or more casts Neutralize Poison on each too (found by CalvinRodo's yuan-ti). Anyone still poisoned when the rest ends dies. The game looked at who was poisoned before those cures, so a 7th-level druid's cure saved no one; the mod casts the cures first (the fix).

Searching junk, hay and wardrobes

Junk piles, haystacks and wardrobes are searched by one of the game's scripts, each with a roll of its own: 0 to N, each number as likely. A low roll finds nothing, and junk and hay stop giving anything once the party has found 6 things in them (each kind counts its own finds); wardrobes count every search, and after 6 every wardrobe is empty.

The Bowyer's archery target

In Teaquetzl, the Bowyer keeps an archery target, and his script decides each shot at it. The distance is the game's, in squares from the archer to the target, and the archer's DEX does the rest:

  • From under 5 squares, every shot is a bull's eye ("could hardly miss at that range"), with no roll and no reward.
  • Otherwise the shot rolls 0 to 20 and hits on a roll under DEX − distance ÷ 2 (rounded down; 2 if DEX is under the distance). Under it by less than 5 is the outer ring, by less than 10 the inner ring, by 10 or more the centre.
  • The centre from more than 13 squares, with the Bowyer within 25 of the archer, has him give 6 Arrows +2, once a game. From 13 squares or closer, the centre is only "the bull's eye".
  • The archer must have a bow in hand, one of the three the script names: the plain bow, the Bow +2 or Phrain's Bow (the mod adds its Dunesong, which the Bowyer sells). With anything else the Bowyer only tells you off.

So a centre from 14 squares needs DEX 17 or more: at DEX 21 a roll of 4 or less (5 in 21), at DEX 18 a 1 or less (2 in 21), at DEX 17 a 0. Each shot is in the dice log with the distances and what it needed.

The arena's count of fights

The game counts the party's trips into Draj's arena (the Loader's combat log shows the count between fights in the slave pens and the arena). It goes up by one each time the party walks out of the arena's gate after being called, however many waves came that day; extra waves don't count. Being called needs the game's clock to run: Kurzak's call comes once a little over 8 hours of game time (8 h 05 min) have passed since the last trip, and the clock stands still while the Look pointer is chosen, so a party resting by the fire with it chosen is never called.

The announcer greets each trip by the count:

How fast the party walks

Everything on the map moves a step at each tick of the game's own clock, 96 a second: the game's timer sets a flag at each tick, and the map's main loop moves everything a step when it finds the flag set, then draws what moved and shows it at the screen's next refresh (70 a second), waiting for it. A tick that passes while a picture is still being drawn and shown sets the flag that is already set, so that step is lost, for every walker at once. Everything on the screen adds to the drawing: four figures rather than the leader alone (the manual's advice to collapse the party "speeds up the game" is this), the mod's gear, shadows and dust, and how fast the computer runs DOSBox. Even the unmodded game loses a step or so for each picture it shows.

The Loader's steady pace keeps the walk near the game's own by leaving out the pictures that would lose too many steps, rather than waiting for them; its section has the pictures a second and the walking speeds measured with it and without.
