Dark Sun: Shattered Lands · Obsidian Edition

The player's guide

How Dark Sun: Shattered Lands plays with the Obsidian Edition mod: making a party, the classes and kits, fighting, magic, psionics, equipment and levels, with the tables you need.

It is spoiler-free: no places, people or quests past the opening arena but those the Loader's Options tab names (the slave pens, Kalzith, Semyon, Dinos's cooked vulture and Scar, in the labels at the end), and no word of where any item is found (the mod's new items are listed by what they are).

Everything here is the mod with its default options, which is how the Loader starts. Nearly every rule change has a switch on the Loader's Options tab (at a glance: each switch, its default and when it takes effect); the exceptions, always on, are the keys (T, S, M, U, I), the fixes to the game's own bugs and the footman's pick. Where it helps to know what a switch changes, a Without the mod note says what the game does with it off. The features page has every rule in full detail, and the Kits page each kit's numbers: the guide links to their tables. The Loader's Help button opens the manual: this guide and the other pages as web pages, in the manual folder.

Getting started

  1. Unzip the mod anywhere and double-click Start Obsidian Edition.bat (the first time, it offers to install Python; see the README).

  2. Press Start the game. The game starts with the mod and your options, and every roll shows as it happens in the Loader's dice log, and in the game itself when you press L:

  3. Make your own party (CREATE CHARACTERS): weapon specs and kits are chosen when a character is made. The game's own four, used when you make none, are given weapon specs to suit them, and with kits on, a kit or features of their own (see Multiclass kits).

Already playing? Saves made before the mod load and play with its fixes and rules, but kits, fuller starting gear and much of the new content come with a new game, and the new gear the game's people carry and its chests hold comes only in areas the save hasn't been to (see your saved games).

Playing

The pointer has three modes, changed with a right-click: Walk, Attack and Look; a left-click acts. Look at someone to Talk, at a thing to Use or Pick Up it, and at a monster in a fight to see its hit points, AC and defences. Resting at a fire ring fills PSP and spell slots; with the mod's Resting as in AD&D the party's priests first cure the party with the spells they have left, and each member heals 3 hit points plus CON's bonus a night, not all of them. What the mod adds to the game's screens and controls is under Quality of life.

Making a party

On the main menu choose CREATE CHARACTERS, right-click an empty slot and choose NEW, then set race, classes, sphere and alignment, roll, and press DONE. Choosing a kit walks through the panel, the mod's KITS page among it. After DONE a warrior picks its weapon specs (weapon specialization), as a preserver picks its spells, and every character then picks its psionic powers (features). A party has one to four characters, all good or neutral.

Ability scores

Each click of the die rolls a whole character:

  • Each score is rolled four times as 4d4 + 4 and the best kept (8 to 20), then the race's adjustment is added.
  • A score below what the classes need is raised to it: each class's least is the manual's (the table below), and a character of several classes takes the highest of each score.
  • What the die gives is what you get. A score can be lowered with a right click, and the points freed raise others with a left click, but the six never add up to more than the die gave. Under CHR, SUM:99/101 is what they add up to now and the most they may: 2 points to spare. A new class that raises a score to its minimum raises the most with it. Without the mod: a click raises any score to its most.
  • STORE and RECALL, under the hit points, keep a roll and bring it back (scores, hit points and PSP), as in Baldur's Gate: store a good roll, try the die again, and recall it if nothing better comes. Changing the race, sex, classes or kit forgets the stored roll.

What the scores do:

  • STR: to-hit and damage in melee, and how much is carried.
  • DEX: AC (−1 at 15, down to −6 at 24), to-hit with missiles, initiative, thief skills, the two-weapon penalty, and dodging fire, cold and electricity (saving throws).
  • CON: hit points, saves against paralysis, poison and death; at 20 or more, a hit point comes back now and then.
  • INT: for preservers, the chance to learn a spell from a scroll and how many spells of each level they can know (learning spells).
  • WIS: priests' bonus spell slots, and saves against mind-affecting magic.

Ages

With Ages and magical aging on, each character has an age from the Dark Sun rules (on the inventory panel, under the saves). Middle, old and venerable age change the scores, and a character dies at the most its race lives. Each 365 days of game time adds a year, and their hair greys on the map as they age (with what the party wears on); the creation screen shows the age a new character starts at. Magic ages them faster: Haste costs a year, after a system shock roll by CON that a character can fail and die (85% to survive at CON 13, 99% from 18); Restore Youth gives magical years back. Without the mod: no ages, and Haste costs nothing.

More than one class

Multiclass (any race but human). A character takes two or three classes at once. Experience is split evenly between them, so each rises more slowly; hit points are shared out (hit points); and the strictest class decides what may be worn and wielded (what each class may use). Dual class (humans only). A human has one class, and can change to another with DUAL on a portrait's right-click menu when:

  • it is 3rd level or more (as the manual says; 2nd with the rule change dual-classing as the manual has it off) and has changed class at most once before;
  • every prime requisite of its current class is 15 or more, and every one of the new class's 17 or more (the table below: a ranger's STR, DEX and WIS, a druid's WIS and CHA, a psionicist's CON and WIS); with the rule off, the game's one score a class (STR for fighters and gladiators, WIS for clerics, druids, psionicists and rangers, INT for preservers, DEX for thieves);
  • the new class is of another group than every class it has had: priests (cleric, druid), warriors (fighter, gladiator, ranger), preserver, psionicist, thief;
  • its alignment suits the new class (a druid true neutral, a ranger good).

The new class starts at 1st level. The old class stops rising, and what it gives (its hit points, THAC0, saves, weapon specs and kit) sleeps until the new class's level passes the old one's; then both count. The new class can take a kit of its own, so a human may end up with three. Some kits rule out classes a human could change to (kits).

The change starts the new class at 0 XP, and any XP past the old level's need is lost: change as soon as the level you want comes. How far the new class can then rise: XP left over at the top.

Fighter

d10 · STR · any race · XP for 2nd level 2,000, for 10th 500,000

How it plays. The steadiest front-liner: any armour, any shield, any weapon, the best THAC0, and it masters one weapon spec. Choose it with care: it is the fighter's weapon for the whole game.

What the mod changes.

  • Weapon specialization: one weapon type, +1 to hit and +2 damage with 3/2 attacks a round; mastery at 5th level (+3 and +3), grand mastery at 9th (the damage die the next size up, against large targets too: a long sword 1d10, 1d20 against a large one; and one more attack). Other weapons attack once a round until 7th level. Without the mod: 3/2 attacks with any weapon, no other bonus.
  • Hit dice rolled twice; levels up to the XP cap.
  • Two weapons cost to-hit, and the off hand attacks once a round.

Weapons and armour. Everything. A fighter with other classes is held to theirs (what each class may use).

Kits.

Myrmidon. Drilled in a sorcerer-king's legions, where a soldier who falls out of step is left for the kanks. They master two weapons where others master one, but years of obeying orders leave their minds open to anyone who barks them, and they wait for the order to strike.

Sentinel. Caravan guards and city-gate wardens who live behind their shields, first to see the raiders coming and last to give ground. They trust bone and obsidian over sorcery, and spells bite them harder for it.

Ravager. Raiders of the wastes who fight half-naked under the crimson sun, scarred hide hardening with every season. They charge in close and stay there: bows, slings and shields are for those who mean to live long.

Each kit's gains, costs and starting gear: Fighter kits on the Kits page.

Starting gear. A plain weapon of its weapon spec (in a material its classes allow), a shield, leather chest, arm and leg armour; with fuller starting gear, also a sling.

More than one class.

  • Multiclass: the combinations and their races are in More than one class.
  • Dual class: with STR 15, a human fighter can become a cleric, druid, preserver, psionicist or thief, never a gladiator or ranger. Its weapon specs sleep with the class; once awake, its specialized weapon specs stay usable whatever the new class allows. Some kits rule out a change of class (which).

Magic weapons. Every one: Dragonsbane +4, Swiftbite +2, El's Drinker +2, Dark Flame +2, Draketooth +1, Hornblade +1, Bloodwrath +1, Flame Blade* +1; Greenbright* +2, Shadowseeker* +1, Mindshard* +1, Stillwater* +1; Dag's Dagger +3, Terror Blade +2, Galefang* +2, Nightsliver* +2, Hayneedle* +1; Glasshewer* +2, Drakejaw* +1, Soulcrusher +1, Axe +1; Great Axe +3, Headsman* +2; Gythka +3, Gythka +2*, Kreenfang* +1; Linebreaker* +2, Polearm +1, Thornwall* +1; Mace +2, Blackmace +1; Quarterstaff +2, Balk's Staff +1, Parting Staff +1; Gutterknot* +1; Deepbiter* +1; Ironwhirl* +2, Cahulaks +2*, Emberlash* +1, Cahulaks +1*; Bow +2, Phrain's Bow +1, Dunesong* +1; Sling +2, Sling +1; Windlash* +1; Skycutter* +2, Chatkcha +1. A Ravager none of the bows, slings, Windlash, chatkchas or Dag's Dagger (thrown).

Gladiator

d10 · STR · any race · XP for 2nd level 2,250, for 10th 600,000

How it plays. The arena's finest: trained in many weapons rather than one, better AC as it rises, and the most varied kits. A gladiator is always a single class (a human may still change class).

What the mod changes.

  • Weapon specialization in two weapon types at 1st level, a third at 6th and a fourth at 9th: +1 to hit, +2 damage and 3/2 attacks with each. Without the mod: 3/2 attacks with any weapon.
  • AC 2 better at 10th level, with the XP cap letting it get there (the game's own AC 1 better from 5th), in armour only. Without the mod: with or without armour.
  • AD&D's experience table. Without the mod: the fighter's.
  • Hit dice rolled twice; levels up to the XP cap.

Weapons and armour. Everything.

Kits.

Arena Champion. Crowd favourites of the fighting pits, schooled to turn every blow with a shield and answer it before the cheering dies. Without one they feel naked on the sand, and fight like it.

Twin-blade. Showfighters who carry a weapon in each hand and make the crowd count the blows. They have no use for a shield, nor for a weapon so heavy it needs both hands.

Brute. Pit fighters bought for size and fed for strength, swinging great axes and mauls that end a bout in one blow. Anything smaller looks like a toy in their hands, and they treat it like one.

Each kit's gains, costs and starting gear: Gladiator kits on the Kits page.

Starting gear. A plain weapon of its first weapon spec, a club in the off hand, leather arm armour; with fuller starting gear, also leather chest and leg armour and a sling.

More than one class.

  • Multiclass: none.
  • Dual class: with STR 15, a human gladiator can become a cleric, druid, preserver, psionicist or thief. Some kits rule out a change of class (which).

Magic weapons. Every one (the fighter's list). A Twin-blade none of the two-handed ones (great axes, gythkas, polearms, quarterstaffs, cahulaks, bows, staff sling); a Brute only Great Axe +3, Headsman*, the gythkas, the polearms, the quarterstaffs and the cahulaks in melee, and any missile weapon.

Ranger

d10 · STR, DEX, WIS · elf, half-elf, half-giant, halfling, human, thri-kreen · good alignment · XP for 2nd level 2,250, for 10th 600,000

How it plays. A warrior of the wastes: the bow and one more weapon spec, two weapons at no penalty in light armour, hiding and moving silently under the open sky to strike from behind, and a little priest magic late on.

What the mod changes.

  • Expertise with the bow and one more weapon spec: a specialist's attacks a round. Without the mod: 3/2 in melee with any weapon.
  • Hiding in shadows in a fight with a thief's numbers for its level, halved indoors; a successful hide and move silently makes its next attack from behind.
  • Priest spells of its sphere from 8th level, cast as a priest of its level less 7 for damage and duration too (1 slot at 8th, 2 at 9th, 2 and 1 at 10th). Without the mod: cast at its full level.
  • AD&D's experience table (2,250 for 2nd level; without the mod: 2,200).
  • Hit dice rolled twice; levels up to the XP cap.

Weapons and armour. Everything; any multiclass ranger keeps the bow whatever its other classes allow.

Kits.

Stalker. Hunters of the scrub and the city's shadowed alleys alike, moving where no one is watching and striking before anyone knows they are there. Heavy armour would only slow them down and give them away.

Seeker. Wanderers on a lifelong pilgrimage through the wastes, looking for the spirit that still lives in Athas's broken land. As they come to understand it, the elements begin to answer them, though only through the arms a priest would carry.

Justifier. Hunters hired to deal with whatever is too dangerous for anyone else: a tribe's chieftain, a defiler's lair, a beast that has emptied a village. They train their weapons to a killer's edge and leave prayer to others.

Each kit's gains, costs and starting gear: Ranger kits on the Kits page.

Starting gear. A plain weapon of its weapon spec, bow and arrows, leather chest and arm armour; with fuller starting gear, also leather leg armour (a Stalker a cloak too).

More than one class.

  • Multiclass: the combinations and their races are in More than one class.
  • Dual class: with STR, DEX and WIS 15, a human ranger can become a cleric, preserver, psionicist or thief (a druid must be neutral, a ranger good). A human of another class with STR, DEX and WIS 17 and a good alignment can become a ranger. Some kits rule out a change of class (which).

Magic weapons. Every one (the fighter's list). A Seeker only its sphere's, and every bow: air, the daggers, the cahulaks, bows, slings, Windlash*, the chatkchas; earth, the metal, obsidian, stone and wooden ones (no bone, no slings); fire, the obsidian ones and the bows; water, the bone and wooden ones.

Thief

d6 · DEX · not half-giant or thri-kreen · XP for 2nd level 1,250, for 10th 160,000

How it plays. Locks, traps and pockets out of a fight; in one, it waits for a clear turn, slips into the shadows and backstabs. Light on hit points, so keep it off the front line.

What the mod changes.

  • Thief skills from AD&D's table with Dark Sun's race and DEX adjustments, none above 95. Without the mod: the game's own, much higher, with penalties for what is held.
  • Hiding in shadows in a fight to backstab, halved in daylight. Without the mod: backstabs only when the target faces someone else. A backstab does ×2 damage at levels 1-4, ×3 at 5-8, ×4 at 9-12 and ×5 at 13 (an Assassin's one step more: ×3 to ×6).
  • Picking pockets with a thief's Pick Pockets button, from anyone in sight.
  • A worn cloak, boots and belt help hiding, moving silently, picking pockets and opening locks.
  • AD&D's experience table (1,250 for 2nd level; without the mod: 1,200).
  • A d6 at 10th too; hit dice rolled twice; levels up to the XP cap.

Weapons and armour. Any weapon, but only one no heavier than a long sword backstabs (see Equipment); light armour only (leather, hide, silk), and of shields only leather ones (the game's own rule), with another class only if that class allows shields. A thief with a warrior or cleric class, or a human once a warrior, may wear heavier armour that class allows, but has no thief skills and no backstab while it does; an Assassin or Shinobi never may, even after changing class.

Kits.

Assassin. Knives for hire in the templars' cities, where a noble house pays well to see a rival fall. They learn to vanish in the full glare of the sun, strike from behind deadlier than any other thief, and leave locks and purses to lesser thieves.

Swashbuckler. Swaggering rogues of the merchant houses and the elven markets, as handy with a blade as any soldier. All that time spent fighting leaves little for the quieter arts.

Shinobi. Spies trained in secret by the Veiled Alliance, who carry a few spells beside their knives. Their masters teach them only what they need, so a scroll is theirs only if they can make out its script, and they travel light. The Effects screen (Current Spell Effects) shows a Shinobi's chance to read a scroll under its kit (the Effects screen's kit line).

Each kit's gains, costs and starting gear: Thief kits, and the Shinobi's slots by level in Kits' spell slots, on the Kits page.

Starting gear. Bone long sword, sling, leather chest armour; with fuller starting gear, also leather arm and leg armour (an Assassin or Shinobi a cloak too).

More than one class.

  • Multiclass: the combinations and their races are in More than one class.
  • Dual class: with DEX 15, a human thief can become a cleric, druid, fighter, gladiator, preserver, psionicist or ranger. Some kits rule out a change of class (which).

Magic weapons. Every one (the fighter's list). A Shinobi only Greenbright*, Shadowseeker*, Mindshard*, Stillwater*, Dag's Dagger, Terror Blade, Galefang*, Nightsliver*, Hayneedle*, the quarterstaffs, bows, slings, Windlash* and the chatkchas.

Preserver

d4 · INT · elf, half-elf, human · XP for 2nd level 2,500, for 10th 250,000

How it plays. The strongest magic and the weakest body: no armour, few weapons, a d4 hit die. Keep it back, act before the enemy reaches it (a character hit in a round can't cast), and choose its spells well.

What the mod changes.

  • INT limits learning: a chance to learn each scroll's spell, and a limit on the spells it knows of each level (learning spells). Without the mod: every scroll learnt, no limit.
  • It chooses its starting spells and one at each level up. EXIT while choosing its starting spells gives it the game's usual ones for the rest.
  • AD&D's wizard spell slots (4 2 1 at 5th level; without the mod: 3 2 1).

Experience and levels

Experience comes from kills (an equal share for each character, split again between a character's classes) and from deeds along the way. A level up comes by itself, with hit points, a better THAC0 and saves, spell slots, a new preserver spell or psionic power, and any weapon spec due.

A class whose prime requisites are all 16 or more (the table) gains 10% more XP from everything, as the manual has it: a fighter with STR 16, a ranger with STR, DEX and WIS 16. The character's own scores count, not what an item or a spell gives. A character of several classes has one XP for them all, so each class with the bonus adds 10% divided by the classes: a fighter/thief with both 10%, with one 5%. A human counts the class it follows now. The Loader adds the bonus as each award comes (the dice log shows it: +12 for Fighter, STR 17), so it needs the Loader following the game; a level that the bonus alone reaches comes with the next award. Fighting

Initiative

At the start of each round everyone's initiative is 20 + a 0-9 roll + DEX's adjustment, +2 Hasted, −2 Slowed, −2 Blind; highest acts first. The weapon makes no difference. Wait drops a character to act later.

A character hit in a round can't cast a spell until the next round: the game won't let it choose a spell, and drops one chosen before the hit. The Battle Mage isn't stopped.

Hitting

An attack hits on d20 ≥ THAC0 − the target's AC. A 20 always hits and a 1 always misses, but there are no critical hits: a 20 does ordinary damage. To-hit changes with:

  • STR in melee, DEX with missiles;
  • the weapon's plus, or a plain weapon's material (weaker materials);
  • weapon specialization;
  • Bless and Prayer +1, Curse −1, Slow −4;
  • attacking from behind +2, a backstab +2 more;
  • two weapons (below).

Damage is the weapon's dice, its plus, specialization's bonus and, in melee, STR's bonus; at least 1.

A held (Paralyzed) or Out Cold target is hit automatically, a 1 too, for normal damage, as in AD&D; a held party member too. Without the mod: the d20 was rolled as ever, and a hit did double damage.

Two weapons

With a melee weapon in each hand:

  • −2 to hit with the main (right) hand and −4 with the off (left) hand, and the DEX adjustment from initiative added, which can lessen the penalty to 0 but never make it a bonus: DEX 17 is 0 and −2, DEX 21 is 0 and 0, DEX 3 is −5 and −7.
  • Rangers, Twin-blades and Battle Dancers have no penalty while all their armour is light (leather, or none); a Justifier has none in any armour, and a Ghetto Fighter's is 0 and −2.
  • The off hand attacks once a round. Extra attacks are the main hand's: a warrior at 3/2 attacks 3/2 + 1 (one round 1 + 1, the next 2 + 1).

A weapon and a shield, or a weapon and a bow or sling, aren't two weapons. Cahulaks, a head in each hand, fight under the same penalties.

Without the mod: no penalty (a small bonus at DEX 5 or less), and each hand attacks at the character's full rate.

From behind, and backstabs

  • Each round a creature turns to face the first one to attack it, and keeps facing that way. An attacker in the square directly behind it attacks from behind: +2 to hit, and the target loses its DEX bonus and its shield.
  • A backstab is a thief's attack from behind, in melee, with a weapon no heavier than a long sword (see Equipment): +4 to hit in all, and the thief's first attack of the round multiplies the weapon's dice ×2 at levels 1-4, ×3 at 5-8, ×4 at 9-12 and ×5 at 13 (an Assassin's one step more); STR's bonus and the weapon's plus are added once after. Without the mod: the whole damage, STR included, is multiplied, on the round's first two attacks.

Hiding in shadows

A thief or ranger whose turn comes in a fight with no enemy in the eight squares around first tries to hide in shadows, then to move silently. With both, their next attack that turn is from behind: a thief's is a backstab with a light enough weapon (not a Military Scout's). Attacking, or ending the turn, gives them away. A soft breath sounds as one hides. An enemy beside them when the turn comes means no hiding: get clear first (a Shadowblade tries anyway).

  • Thieves' hiding is halved in daylight (under the open sky; not an Assassin's, a Military Scout's or Cilla's); rangers' is halved indoors instead.
  • A worn cloak adds 10 to hiding in shadows and worn boots 10 to moving silently, up to 95. A worn belt adds 5 to a thief's picking pockets and opening locks. The inventory screen's thief skills count them.

Without the mod: thieves backstab only a target that has turned to face someone else, and no one hides in a fight.

Monsters

Monsters have defences of their own: some need magic weapons, some are hurt only by edged weapons or immune to crushing ones, some shrug off fire, cold, poison or the powers of the mind, some can't be charmed or held. How spells work

  • Damage grows with the caster's level up to 10th: Fireball and Lightning Bolt do at most 10d6. Someone Out Cold gets no save and takes the most the dice can do.
  • Duration is so much a caster level plus dice; a round is a minute of game time.
  • Caster level is the caster's best level in a class whose sphere has the spell. A ranger casts at its level less 7, for what it may cast and for damage and duration alike. An elemental spell works only for a priest of that element.
  • Area spells catch the caster and the party.
  • Hold Person, Charm Person and Charm Person or Mammal don't work on a large target (a half-giant, a large monster, anyone Enlarged), with no save; Charm Person or Mammal still charms a large beast (size). Without the mod: the game keeps no sizes.
  • Spells roll to hit (a rule change, on by default). The missile spells, Acid Arrow, Turn Pebble to Boulder, Magical Stone, Condense and each of Minute Meteors' five globes (at +2), roll a d20 against the target's AC as a weapon would (Acid Arrow with a fighter's THAC0 of the caster's level, the rest with the caster's own, and DEX's missile adjustment), and so does a touch spell cast at an enemy (Chill Touch, Shocking Grasp, Vampiric Touch, the Cause Wounds spells, Cause Disease, Poison), with STR's. A 20 always hits, a 1 always misses, and a miss does nothing: "Miss" floats over the target. Monsters' spines roll too. Without the mod: they always landed.
  • Spells and psionics as the manual describes them (a rule change, on by default): twenty-four spells and two psionic powers, and seven monsters, as the manual has them (Solid Fog for a few rounds, not the whole fight; Hold Undead only on undead of the caster's level or lower; Strength by class; shadows that drain STR; the list is in the features page).
  • Turn Undead, the cleric's button on the USE screen, follows AD&D's turning table (with the manual's spells): it tries each undead in its area, one at a time; by the undead's hit dice and the cleric's level, a d20 turns it (it flees for 3d4 rounds), or it is turned or destroyed with no roll, or can't be turned at all. A cleric who fails against an undead can't try it again that fight. Without the mod: each success destroyed, and a failure could be tried again and again.
  • Resting heals by the priests' spells left and a night's rest, not to full: Resting.
  • Mirror Image's images take each hit, and each foe's spell aimed at one creature, three times in four (Reflected Image's half the time), the image vanishing and the blow or spell lost; your own side's spells always get through. Without the mod: the images stopped no weapon blow, and took the caster's own Armor.
  • Armor uses a charge for each damaging hit. Without the mod: two for each hit, and one for any spell landing on its wearer, a friend's Bless too.
  • Share Strength ends on its receiver when the power ends. Without the mod: the receiver kept the strength.
  • Dispel Magic only dispels. Without the mod: the preserver's also stopped everyone it caught casting spells, your own party too.
  • Negative Plane Protection gives a save against an undead's draining touch; saved, the touch is stopped and the undead takes 2d6. Without the mod: it never stopped anything.
  • Cloak of Bravery gives +4 on saves against fear spells. Without the mod: the bonus never applied.
  • Absorb Disease takes the disease only. Without the mod: it gave the psionicist STR too.
  • Cause Serious and Critical Wounds do 2d8+1 and 3d8+3, as the Cure spells heal (Light: 1d8 both ways). Without the mod: 2d9 and 3d9.
  • Enlarge adds 10% a caster level to the weapon's dice only, rounded up; STR and magic are added after. Without the mod: to the whole melee damage, STR included.
  • Shillelagh, Flame Blade and Spiritual Hammer need an empty hand. With weapon specialization on, a spec counts with them as with a club, a long sword and a mace.
  • Six new wizard spells, learnt like any other:
    • Cat's Grace (2nd level, on a party member): DEX up by a die that depends on the class (a rogue 1d8; a wizard, warrior or psionicist 1d6; a priest 1d4; a multiclass character's best; at most 24) for an hour of game time a caster level, which betters AC, initiative, thief skills and dodging. Without the manual's spells: DEX + 1d6 for anyone.
    • Erdlu Claw (1st level, on a party member): talons for 5 rounds a caster level. Unarmed attacks, a thri-kreen's claws but not its bite, do 1d6+1 where that's better, are +1 to hit and count as a +1 magical edged weapon (a thri-kreen's claws and bite cut even without it). With Body Weaponry or Animal Affinity, their dice are +1 (2d4+1, 1d10+1).
    • Fogbolt (1st level): a dark grey bolt of clinging fog at one creature in a clear line, for 2d4 rounds unless it saves: −3 to its attacks, +2 to attacks on it.
    • Reflected Image (1st level, on the caster): one image, which a hit or a foe's spell strikes half the time instead of the caster, and which then vanishes. It and Mirror Image replace each other; Dispel Magic ends it.
    • Spook (1st level): one creature flees in fear for 3 rounds unless it saves, at −1 for every 2 caster levels (at most −6). Undead aren't affected; magic resistance applies.
    • Friends (1st level, on the caster): CHA + 2d4 (at most 25) for 1d4 rounds and 1 a caster level, for better prices. Casting it again replaces it.

Resting

Use a fire ring (the Look pointer on it, out of a fight) to rest 8 hours. The game then fills every member's spell slots and PSP, brings STR back (but a diseased member's), and a cleric or druid of 5th level or more among those Okay casts Cure Disease, Remove Curse and Remove Paralysis on everyone, a druid of 7th or more Neutralize Poison too. Anyone still Poisoned when the rest ends dies.

With Resting as in AD&D (a rule change, on by default), the rest heals as AD&D's does:

  1. The priests' spells first. Before the slots fill again, each priest who can cast spends the priest spells it has left on the party: Neutralize Poison on the Poisoned, Cure Blindness on the Blind, Cure Disease on the Diseased (when no cleric or druid of 5th level would cure it free), then Cure Light, Serious and Critical Wounds and Heal on the most wounded, the cure that fits what the night won't heal. So a 4th-level slot kept back keeps a poisoned friend alive.
  2. Then psionicists' Cell Adjustment (5 PSP each: 1d4 HP and a disease cured), on the Diseased, then the wounded, while their PSP last.
  3. A night's natural healing: each member heals 3 hit points plus CON's hit point bonus (its bonus a level, hit points: a warrior's in full, +1 at CON 15 up to +7, anyone else's at most +2), up to its most. CON 20 or more also regenerates through the 8 hours.

So rest more than once to heal a badly hurt party with no priest, or keep cure spells back for the night. Untick the box for the game's rest.

With a bow in the missile slot, a click on a foe two squares away swings the polearm; from three on, the bow shoots. Without the mod: a polearm strikes only an adjacent foe.

Armour is worn in pieces (chest, arms, legs, a helm), each lowering AC by its own amount, and a shield and DEX lower it more (but not against an attack from behind). Helms give AC 1. Without the mod: helms give no AC. The inventory screen's AC line counts them all; the Loader's Characters tab shows the AC of the last attack and what made it.

Protection. Rings and cloaks of protection give +1 AC and +1 on every save, by AD&D's rules: two rings don't add up; a ring gives no AC with magical armour (its saves still count); a cloak does nothing with magical or metal armour or a shield. A cloak isn't armour: any class may wear one, druids too. Bracers of defense give their AC (6, 5, 4 or 2), and the mod's two robes their plus, only with no armour worn, and a preserver casts in either. These are rare finds.

Acid. Some monsters' acid or corroding touch can destroy worn armour or a held weapon. Each item gets a saving throw by its material: wood 8, bone 11, stone and obsidian 5, metal 13, leather 10, cloth 12, less its plus (a weapon uses the game's own number where that's easier). Without the mod: plain armour is destroyed outright.

Worth knowing

  • Cure poison before resting. A character still Poisoned when time passes out of a fight dies (with Resting as in AD&D, a priest's Neutralize Poison left unspent is cast at the rest: resting).
  • Area spells catch the caster and the party. Aim fireballs and clouds away from your own.
  • Act before you are hit if you mean to cast (why).
  • Someone Out Cold takes the most a spell's dice can do, with no save.
  • Two weapons cost to-hit unless your DEX is high or you are a ranger, Twin-blade or Battle Dancer in light armour, and the off hand attacks only once a round.
  • A held or Out Cold foe is hit by every blow, for normal damage.
  • A warrior's weapon specs matter: outside them it attacks once a round until 7th level.
  • Multiclass preservers take off their armour to cast (a helm counts), and multiclass thieves to use their thief skills (light armour is fine).
  • Thieves hide with no enemy beside them when their turn comes; step away first. Daylight halves their chance, and rangers' indoors.
  • Plain wood, bone, stone and obsidian weapons can break (clubs and quarterstaffs can't); magic ones never do.
  • Spell damage stops growing at caster level 10.
  • Wait puts a character later in the round.
  • Ready a missile weapon and a melee weapon: the game uses whichever fits the target's distance.
  • The look pointer pauses the game. While it is up, no time passes: no one moves, and no one comes calling. Switch back to the walk arrow to let time run.
  • The people you fight wear their armour from the start. Without the mod they put it on only when they first acted in a fight, so an opening blow or a backstab before then met AC 10; now it meets their armour.

The Options tab at a glance

Every switch on the Loader's Options tab, in the tab's order and under its sections, with the words the Loader gives it. They count in games started from the Loader or its .bat files. Restore defaults, at the bottom of the tab, sets every switch back to the default below.

Takes effect says when a change counts:

  • at once: in the game running, as soon as the box is ticked or unticked;
  • next start: the next time the game is started (the Loader writes it into the game's copy as it starts it; the label says "from the next start");
  • new characters: characters made from then on, while those already made keep what they have;
  • first visit: from the next start, in an area the party hasn't visited yet. A saved game keeps what it already has (people met, items given); a new game has it all.

Some changes have no switch and are always on: the keys T and Shift+T (looting), S (selling), M, U and I (controls), what the game's own screens show (always on), the fixes to the game's own bugs and the footman's pick's 1d6+1 (creature size).
